- Strongest aspects
- This is now a strong and coherent referee-facing outline. The proposal has one clearly defined core measurement, one sharply bounded physics-use validation, and one disciplined contingency structure. That gives it both scientific identity and execution credibility.
- The real-data CMS framing is now right. The anchor measurement is presented as a recognizable CMS `ttbar(2l)` analysis with standard objects, backgrounds, calibrations, and nuisance classes, with the novelty sitting in the unbinned inference layer. That is exactly the right way to make an ambitious method look experimentally fundable.
- The Higgs component is now appropriately disciplined. Choosing differential `ggH` in `H -> gamma gamma` as the named validation target gives the program a concrete and plausible downstream payoff without turning Higgs into a second flagship.
- The restricted-gluon claim is much more honest and defensible than earlier versions. The outline now states clearly that the fit constrains the gluon component in a restricted model space, uses standard external quark inputs in the baseline, and activates the Drell-Yan control only if robustness tests show that it is needed.
- The stage structure is strong. The progression from pseudo-data closure, to real-data validation, to the real-data constrained-gluon result, to the Higgs validation reads like an executable research program rather than a conceptual manifesto.
- The explicit publication logic is especially effective. Stating directly that publication 1 is the real-data `ttbar(2l)` constrained-gluon result and publication 2 is the `H -> gamma gamma` validation only if publication 1 is secure makes the proposal look unusually well controlled.

- Remaining weaknesses
- At this point the main risk is no longer conceptual confusion but loss of discipline in later rewriting. The proposal is strongest in its current narrow form and should resist adding further beneficiary channels, software ambitions, or broader PDF rhetoric.
- The anchor measurement remains a very large task. That is acceptable, but only if the text keeps making clear that publication 1, the real-data CMS `ttbar(2l)` constrained-gluon result, is by itself a full success condition for the grant.
- Theory accuracy and modeling dependence still sit on the critical path. The outline now treats them properly, but they remain one of the few places where the scientific case could weaken if phrased too lightly in the full proposal.

- Structural recommendations
- Keep the current hierarchy exactly as it is: core measurement, method validation, physics exploitation, with only one visible stress test and one contingency control input.
- Keep the publication logic explicit. It is unusually effective in making the proposal look realistic: publication 1 is the `ttbar(2l)` constrained-gluon result; publication 2 is the `H -> gamma gamma` validation only if publication 1 is secure.
- Keep the contingency wording exactly this tight. "Only two visible follow-ons" is the right amount of optionality: one high-mass `ttbar` stress test plus one only-if-needed Drell-Yan control input.
- Compress rather than expand. The proposal now benefits more from tightening prose and removing repetition than from adding new ideas.

- What to cut
- Cut any residual language that could reopen the appearance of a broad multi-channel platform project.
- Cut any phrasing that suggests public likelihood release is a central guaranteed output of the proposal period rather than a strategic downstream possibility.

- What to emphasize
- Emphasize that the strongest feature is concentrated ambition: one hard real-data CMS measurement with a clearly defined physics payoff.
- Emphasize that the proposal is no longer selling "ML for HEP" in the abstract, but a specific new route to extracting proton-structure information from rich CMS detector-level events.
- Emphasize that the proposal wins by focus: realistic experiment, calibrated claims, and a memorable top-to-gluon-to-Higgs logic.

- Bottom-line judgment
- This is now in good shape. The outline is scientifically differentiated, experimentally more credible, and much better scoped than the early versions.
- It now reads as a final-round outline rather than a draft still searching for its center.
- The correct final move is restraint. Do not broaden it again; let the proposal succeed on clarity, discipline, and the strength of the anchor measurement.
