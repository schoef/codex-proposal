Joint ttbar + ggH(H->gammagamma) fit: tightened uncertainty map
1. True shared nuisances are only those generated by the same latent inputs in both sectors: the gluon-PDF coefficients by construction, and optionally a consistently propagated alpha_s choice; luminosity is operationally shared within one experiment/run, while beam-energy effects are shared but usually subleading.
2. Merely analogous, not shared: ttbar and ggH each have perturbative scale, shower/resummation, acceptance, and missing-higher-order systematics, but these arise in different calculations and should be separate nuisance blocks even if their taxonomy looks similar.
3. Fully separate detector/background effects: ttbar detector nuisances are JES/JER, b tagging, leptons, MET, combinatorics; H->gammagamma detector nuisances are photon scale/resolution, photon ID/isolation, and vertexing; none should be assumed to cancel across channels.
4. The ggH continuum diphoton background is not a side nuisance but a structural component of the Higgs-side likelihood: in an unbinned fit it enters the same event density as signal in m_gg and any auxiliary observables, and its shape/normalization parameters should stay H-channel-specific unless a theory-driven transfer model is built explicitly.
5. Category-migration nuisances are also structural, not cosmetic: photon-energy scale/resolution, jet thresholds, and vertex assignment move signal and background events between Higgs categories, so the migration model must be embedded in the response map rather than profiled as a late normalization uncertainty.
6. Current Higgs inclusive precision is still statistics-led: CMS quotes sigma_fid = 74 +- 11 (stat) +5/-4 (syst) fb at 13.6 TeV in H->gammagamma (CMS HIG-23-014 / JHEP 09 (2025) 070), so detector/background nuisances are important but not yet the dominant global limiter.
7. For ggH signal theory, cautious sizing is best: inclusive fiducial prediction uncertainty is at the several-percent level in the CMS result (67.8 +- 3.8 fb, same source), while harder pT_H tails are widely understood to be substantially less stable because scale variation, matching/resummation, and finite-m_t effects grow.
8. For ttbar, recent differential measurements show detector systematics at a few percent in central bins and much larger modeling uncertainty in extreme tails; ATLAS reports typical 2-5% systematics with growth toward ~20% at high pT (Eur. Phys. J. C 79 (2019) 1028), which is the right cautionary scale for aggressive PDF extraction.
9. Priority ranking for a first joint fit: (i) shared gluon-PDF directions, (ii) ttbar modeling, (iii) ggH continuum-background plus category-migration structure, (iv) ggH high-pT theory if those bins are used aggressively, (v) channel-specific detector nuisances, (vi) luminosity/beam inputs.
10. Minimal staged implementation: share only PDF coefficients, maybe alpha_s, and luminosity; keep all theory systematics process-specific; build the Higgs likelihood as signal-plus-continuum over m_gg x {y_H,pT_H,category}; only promote an extra nuisance to “shared” if a common generative origin can be written down, not because the labels look alike.
