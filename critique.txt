1. What is strongest and worth protecting

- The plan is much stronger now that it has a single baseline chain: `m_tt` as the top input, normalized `|y_H|` as the Higgs target, and one rank-1 gluon-PDF mode as the transferable object. That is finally a real research skeleton rather than a platform pitch.
- The decision to treat this as an identifiability and closure paper, not a guaranteed precision-gain paper, is the right move. It makes negative outcomes scientifically usable.
- Choosing `|y_H|` rather than `pT(H)` is a defensible first target. It isolates the proton-structure transfer question from recoil and radiation-model complications.
- The explicit validity criteria are a major improvement, especially the statement that sequential transfer fails if hidden extra tuning is needed on the Higgs side.

2. What is weak, vague, or overreaching - and cannot yet be claimed

- The central unresolved issue is not originality anymore; it is sufficiency. The plan still assumes that one rank-1 physical mode is enough until proven otherwise. That is a useful hypothesis, but it is not yet a justified baseline fact.
- The current thresholds look convenient rather than principled. `>= 30%` Higgs-variance capture, `>= 20%` posterior-width contraction, and `10-20%` reduction of the aligned Higgs component may be reasonable working numbers, but the plan does not explain why crossing them would constitute success and missing them would constitute failure. Right now they read like tuning knobs.
- The sequential story is improved, but there is still a hidden joint-fit assumption in the validity audit. The line "sequential and joint closure results agree within uncertainty" is correct as a test, but it implicitly acknowledges that the sequential parameterization may not be sufficient unless the omitted shared nuisance structure is already benign. That means the paper is not yet fully protected against the criticism that the sequential result is only a projection of a problem that is really joint.
- "Physical interpretation" of the rank-1 mode is still underspecified. A mode being stable under SVD-like compression and concentrated in overlapping `x-Q^2` support does not yet prove it is a uniquely meaningful gluon-shape direction rather than the leading artifact of the chosen binning and prior.

3. Which claims need evidence or a tighter plan

- Claim needing evidence: one rank-1 mode is enough for the baseline.
Why it matters: if the leading Higgs-relevant uncertainty is split across two comparably important directions, the whole minimal paper changes character. A rank-1-only baseline would then risk reporting an artifact of over-compression rather than a true boundary.
Better move: add a pre-declared rank-1 sufficiency test. For example, require a noticeable eigengap or require that rank-2 adds only a small incremental captured Higgs variance and does not qualitatively change the sequential posterior. Without that, rank-1 is a choice, not a result.

- Claim needing evidence: the sequential `ttbar -> |y_H|` baseline is self-contained.
Why it matters: the plan is trying to avoid a hidden dependence on a broader simultaneous treatment. If there remain shared nuisances or theory choices that only become visible in a joint setup, then the sequential paper is not yet methodologically closed.
Better move: define explicitly what is allowed to be shared between top and Higgs in the audit. If common PDF freedom is the only transported degree of freedom, then all other shared ingredients must either be fixed, marginalized independently, or shown to be numerically negligible. Otherwise the sequential validity test is too soft.

- Claim needing evidence: the chosen thresholds represent meaningful scientific decisions.
Why it matters: arbitrary thresholds weaken the whole paper because a skeptic can say the verdict was chosen after seeing the geometry.
Better move: justify the thresholds from one of three sources: expected effect sizes from prior studies, numerical distinguishability from zero given realistic pseudo-data, or a simple decision-theoretic rule such as "large enough to matter relative to total Higgs uncertainty." If that cannot be done, present them explicitly as provisional screening thresholds, not success criteria.

- Claim needing evidence: normalized `m_tt` and normalized `|y_H|` preserve enough sensitivity to the same physical direction.
Why it matters: normalization may remove exactly the luminosity-like information that makes the transfer look clean in theory space.
Better move: state whether the baseline claim is really about shape-only transport, or whether absolute information is needed on the top side. The plan currently mixes absolute and normalized `m_tt` in Stage 0 without resolving this.

4. Risk assessment of the newest or boldest ideas

- Major idea: one rank-1 physical PDF mode
Main risk: the leading common direction is not dominant enough to justify rank-1 closure, and rank-2 effects are not negligible.
Is the risk acceptable: yes, but only if the paper treats rank-1 as a falsifiable minimal ansatz rather than the presumed truth.
What would de-risk it: a rank-1 versus rank-2 comparison stated up front, with a hard rule for when rank-1 is deemed insufficient.

- Major idea: sequential `ttbar -> |y_H|` baseline
Main risk: hidden shared nuisances or theory correlations make the apparent transfer depend on assumptions that only a joint construction exposes.
Is the risk acceptable: yes, because this is exactly the methodological point worth testing.
What would de-risk it: a very explicit statement of the factorization assumption and a matched pseudo-data audit showing whether omitted shared structure changes the Higgs posterior materially.

- Major idea: `|y_H|` as the first Higgs target
Main risk: it is so clean that it becomes too weak, yielding an elegant but practically negligible result.
Is the risk acceptable: yes. For a first paper, "clean but weak" is better than "stronger but confounded."
What would de-risk it: declare in advance that modest gain is acceptable if the closure logic is sharp, and do not oversell eventual impact on full Higgs systematics.

5. What the proposer should do next

- Add one essential fix before declaring the plan finished: a rank-1 sufficiency criterion. The plan currently says how to build the rank-1 mode, but not how to decide whether rank-1 is actually enough. That is the main missing piece.
- Make the hidden joint-fit issue fully explicit. State in one place what non-PDF correlations are assumed absent, frozen, or negligible in the sequential baseline, and what discrepancy with a matched joint closure would count as failure.
- Recast the numerical thresholds as either justified criteria or provisional screening numbers. In the current form they are too arbitrary to carry the paper.
- Resolve the absolute-versus-normalized top input ambiguity. If absolute `m_tt` is needed to learn the mode, say so. If normalized `m_tt` is the true baseline, then defend why it still probes the intended gluon-luminosity direction.

Bottom line

- The plan is not bloated anymore and is close to being strong enough to stop. But it is still missing one essential fix: an explicit test for whether rank-1 is sufficient rather than merely convenient.
- If that fix is added, the baseline becomes a coherent, defensible minimum paper. Without it, the proposal still leaves too much room for the criticism that the result was created by compression choices and protected by an implicit joint-fit backstop.
