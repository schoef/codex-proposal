1. What is strongest and worth protecting

- The revised plan is much closer to a defensible stopping point. It now has a real minimal chain: normalized `m_tt` as the control input, normalized `|y_H|` as the target, and one explicitly defined shared gluon-PDF mode as the transported object.
- The best improvement is that the paper is no longer framed as "top helps Higgs" in the abstract. It is framed as a falsifiable identifiability-and-closure test of a very specific sequential claim.
- The explicit statement that common nuisances must either be propagated or excluded is important. That is the right instinct and should be kept.
- Treating negative outcomes as publishable boundary results remains a strength. That protects the project from scope creep if rank-1 transfer fails.

2. What is weak, vague, or overreaching and cannot yet be claimed

- The revised rank-1 baseline is close to defensible, but not yet fully defensible to stop. The remaining weakness is not general sprawl anymore; it is that the stopping rules still look partly chosen for convenience.
- The sufficiency tolerances (`0.25 sigma` mean agreement, `10%` variance agreement, `10%` predictive-score degradation) are better than the earlier ad hoc impact numbers, but they still need a sharper rationale. Right now they read as reasonable engineering thresholds, not as clearly motivated scientific decision rules.
- The shared-nuisance boundary is improved in language, but still not operational enough. "Explicitly modeled common nuisances if any are kept" is too soft. A critic can still ask whether the sequential closure only works because the really dangerous common structure was left outside the game.
- The normalized-versus-absolute `m_tt` choice is cleaner than before, but it is not yet fully intellectually settled. The plan says the paper is about a shared gluon-luminosity shape direction while also saying normalization is used to suppress overall-rate contamination. That is plausible, but it needs to be shown rather than asserted, because normalization can also erase part of the physically useful PDF lever arm.

3. Which claims need evidence or a tighter plan

- Claim needing tighter support: the declared sufficiency thresholds are enough to justify stopping at rank-1.
Why it matters: if those tolerances are too loose, rank-1 can "pass" even when higher-rank structure is still scientifically relevant; if too tight, rank-1 is rejected for cosmetic reasons.
Better move: tie each threshold to the actual scientific claim. For example, explain that `0.25` of the reference posterior sigma is the largest bin-level bias that still leaves the sequential posterior practically interchangeable with the reference for the purpose of the baseline conclusion. If that argument cannot be made, label the thresholds as provisional screening tolerances rather than baseline-validation criteria.

- Claim needing tighter support: the shared-nuisance boundary is explicit enough for a sequential claim.
Why it matters: this is the main route by which a skeptic will say the sequential result is a dressed-up projection of a joint problem. If luminosity, acceptance, unfolding, theory calibration, or perturbative choices induce residual cross-channel correlation, then `y_H ⟂ y_t | z` is not actually the operative baseline.
Better move: add a short explicit table with three columns: top-only, Higgs-only, common. For each common nuisance class, state one of two actions: propagated in the latent state, or excluded from the baseline claim and demonstrated negligible for the chosen normalized observables.

- Claim needing tighter support: normalized `m_tt` is the coherent baseline, not merely the cleanest-looking one.
Why it matters: the whole baseline now rests on the idea that shape-only `m_tt` still constrains the same shared gluon direction that matters for normalized `|y_H|`. If the most informative part of the transfer sits in the absolute rate component, then the normalization choice has made the baseline artificially elegant but physically incomplete.
Better move: require one early diagnostic that compares absolute and normalized `m_tt` in the response geometry. The paper does not need absolute `m_tt` as a co-baseline, but it does need to show that the chosen rank-1 mode remains materially visible after normalization and is not mostly a removed normalization direction.

4. Risk assessment of the newest or boldest ideas

- Major idea: stopping at a rank-1 baseline with the current tolerances
Main risk: the thresholds certify convenience rather than sufficiency.
Is the risk acceptable: conditionally yes.
What would de-risk it: justify the tolerances from interchangeability with the reference posterior, or present a sensitivity study showing the qualitative verdict is stable when the thresholds are tightened and loosened within a reasonable range.

- Major idea: an explicit shared-nuisance boundary for sequential transfer
Main risk: the boundary is nominal rather than real, and omitted common effects are what would actually spoil the factorization.
Is the risk acceptable: yes, because the plan now at least acknowledges the issue directly.
What would de-risk it: freeze a baseline nuisance partition before closure studies and add one matched joint-versus-sequential stress test whose only purpose is to detect missing common structure.

- Major idea: normalized rather than absolute `m_tt` as the defining baseline observable
Main risk: the baseline becomes coherent statistically but too sanitized physically, because the most transferable PDF information may partly live in the overall normalization component.
Is the risk acceptable: yes, but only if the paper is explicit that it is testing shape-transfer sufficiency, not total top-derived PDF information.
What would de-risk it: show that normalized `m_tt` still captures a material fraction of the Higgs-relevant shared mode and that switching to absolute `m_tt` does not qualitatively change which gluon direction is being learned.

5. What the proposer should do next

- Keep the revised rank-1 baseline. It is now coherent enough that the proposer should resist adding channels or observables.
- Add one paragraph that justifies the sufficiency tolerances as decision rules rather than convenient numbers. If a principled justification is not available, downgrade them explicitly to working tolerances and commit to a threshold-sensitivity appendix.
- Make the shared-nuisance boundary fully explicit. A compact nuisance partition table is enough. The goal is to leave no ambiguity about what is common, what is propagated, and what is being declared outside the baseline claim.
- Add one early comparison demonstrating that normalized `m_tt` is not throwing away the very mode the paper wants to study. This can be a response-geometry comparison to absolute `m_tt`; it does not require expanding the baseline.
- State the baseline claim in the narrowest defensible form: the paper tests whether shape-level top information, compressed into one shared gluon mode, is an approximately sufficient sequential summary for normalized `|y_H|` once the declared nuisance boundary is enforced.

Bottom line

- The revised baseline is almost defensible to stop. The scope is now disciplined, and the rank-1 story is no longer obviously decorative.
- It still needs three final hardenings before it is truly safe to freeze: justify the sufficiency thresholds, make the shared-nuisance boundary operational, and prove that normalized rather than absolute `m_tt` is a coherent baseline choice rather than just the tidiest one.
- If those three points are tightened, the proposer should stop expanding and execute the program.
