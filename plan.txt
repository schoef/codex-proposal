1. Core thesis

- Main claim: build a CMS analysis and interpretation program in which top-quark data act as an in-situ control system for the gluon PDF, represented through a shared latent constraint, and propagate that constraint directly into differential Higgs measurements.
- Scientific point: the novelty is not "better top" or "better Higgs" separately, but a common proton-structure layer inferred from one sector and consumed by another.
- Concrete scope choice: the baseline program focuses on gluon-sensitive observables in top-pair production and gluon-fusion Higgs production. Single-top, inclusive jets, and SMEFT are staged extensions, not baseline requirements.
- Deliverable: a validated workflow that takes measured top observables, infers a low-dimensional latent description of the gluon-PDF uncertainty relevant to CMS kinematics, and uses it to sharpen Higgs differential predictions and uncertainty decomposition.

2. Scope and architectural choices

- Baseline physics scope
- Use top-pair production as the primary constraint channel because it is directly gluon-dominated at the LHC and already has rich differential information.
- Use differential Higgs observables in ggF as the primary target because they inherit gluon-PDF sensitivity in a way that is scientifically clean and immediately useful.
- Restrict the first proposal stage to Standard Model QCD+EW theory systematics and PDF structure. Do not mix SMEFT into the baseline inference.

- Why this scope belongs together
- Top-pair and ggF Higgs probe overlapping x and Q2 regions but in different final states and scales; the shared latent layer is exactly the object that turns this overlap into a research program.
- The program is differentiated from standard PDF profiling because it is detector-facing, analysis-integrated, and explicitly cross-channel rather than a generic global-fit exercise.

- What is deliberately out of baseline scope
- Single-top enters only after the baseline latent-constraint machinery is stable.
- Inclusive jets enter only as an optional high-leach, high-risk auxiliary channel.
- SMEFT enters only after Standard Model closure tests show that the latent PDF layer is not absorbing obvious missing-physics effects.

3. Central research questions

- RQ1: Can CMS top differential data be compressed into a low-dimensional latent constraint that captures the part of the gluon-PDF uncertainty relevant for Higgs observables?
- RQ2: Does this shared latent constraint improve differential Higgs precision in a way that is robust across theory choices, unfolding choices, and observable bases?
- RQ3: Which top observables carry the most transferable information to Higgs, and where does transfer fail because of phase-space or modeling mismatch?
- RQ4: When do additional channels such as single-top and jets add genuine orthogonal information, and when do they mainly import modeling risk?
- RQ5: Can the framework be extended to SMEFT without conflating proton-structure uncertainty with BSM shape effects?

4. Core thesis A: shared latent gluon-PDF constraint from top to Higgs

- A1. Construct the latent representation
- Define a low-dimensional latent basis for the gluon PDF uncertainty relevant to CMS kinematics. Practical options:
- principal components of Hessian/replica PDF variations restricted to the x-Q2 region populated by top and Higgs observables,
- an analysis-level nuisance basis obtained by response-matrix compression of PDF variations into observable space,
- or a hybrid basis chosen to maximize transferability from top to Higgs.
- Requirement: the latent basis must be interpretable enough to validate and small enough to profile in realistic fits.

- A2. Learn the constraint from top data
- Fit the latent parameters using top-pair observables measured by CMS.
- Compare binned profiling and simulation-assisted unbinned likelihood/compression only if it clearly improves sensitivity; baseline deliverable should remain possible with standard binned measurements.
- Separate statistical, detector, scale, and PDF-induced effects so the latent layer is not just soaking up generic mismodeling.

- A3. Propagate to differential Higgs
- Push the posterior or profiled latent parameters into Higgs differential predictions.
- Quantify reduction in theory uncertainty, shifts in central values, and changes in correlation patterns across Higgs bins.
- Measure the scientific payoff not only by smaller error bars, but by cleaner uncertainty attribution and stronger cross-channel consistency tests.

- A4. Establish closure and transportability
- Verify that a latent basis fit on top observables reproduces independent gluon-sensitive theory variations in Higgs predictions.
- Test whether transport works only for integrated rates or also for shape information at high pT and large invariant mass.

5. Subtasks for core thesis A

- Theory and simulation layer
- Produce top and Higgs predictions with consistent PDF sets, perturbative settings, and scale-variation bookkeeping.
- Map each observable bin to effective x-Q2 support to justify why transfer is expected.
- Build fast interpolation or reweighting infrastructure so latent-parameter scans are computationally feasible.

- Observable design layer
- Top baseline observables:
- ttbar: m_tt, pT(t), y_tt, pT(tt), HT, and optionally double-differential bins such as m_tt vs y_tt or pT(t) vs y(t).
- Prioritize observables with strong gluon leverage and manageable modeling systematics.
- Higgs target observables:
- pT(H), N_jets in ggF categories, y_H, pT(H)+jets, and high-pT tails where gluon-PDF uncertainty and top-induced transfer may matter most.
- Keep the first target set small enough that covariance propagation is interpretable.

- Statistical layer
- Implement a joint likelihood or sequential profiling workflow with latent nuisance parameters shared across channels.
- Compare two strategies:
- sequential: constrain latent parameters with top, then propagate to Higgs;
- simultaneous: build a combined likelihood with shared latent nuisances.
- Baseline milestone should deliver the sequential version first because it is easier to validate and publish.

- Validation layer
- Closure on pseudo-data generated from held-out PDF replicas/eigenvectors.
- Stress tests under scale variations, top-mass variations, shower/hadronization changes, and alternative unfolding choices.
- Null tests with observables expected to have weak gluon sensitivity.

6. Other core thesis B: identify the best top observables for transferable gluon information

- Purpose: avoid a diffuse "use all top data" program and instead produce a ranked, physics-driven basis of control observables.
- B1. Sensitivity audit
- For each candidate top observable, estimate sensitivity to latent gluon modes and nuisance degeneracies.
- Rank observables by transfer value to Higgs, not just by standalone top precision.
- B2. Redundancy audit
- Quantify which observables are nearly redundant after accounting for covariance and acceptance effects.
- Use this to define a compact top control set for the baseline proposal.
- B3. Phase-space matching study
- Identify where the top-constrained x-Q2 region overlaps Higgs and where extrapolation begins.
- This gives a principled statement of where the method is solid and where it becomes exploratory.

- Relation to thesis A
- Thesis B makes thesis A operational: without observable ranking and phase-space matching, the shared latent idea stays too vague for a proposal.

7. Other core thesis C: channel extensions with explicit staging

- C1. Single-top extension
- Motivation: single-top adds sensitivity to quark-gluon initial states and offers a handle on whether the latent gluon basis remains meaningful outside ttbar-dominated kinematics.
- Proposed observables:
- t-channel top pT and y, reconstructed m_tj or forward-jet observables, and inclusive/differential ST cross sections in phase space with controlled acceptance.
- Scientific role:
- not primarily to tighten the baseline gluon constraint,
- but to test robustness of the latent transport picture and expose quark-gluon contamination or electroweak-modeling dependencies.
- Entry condition:
- only pursue after baseline top-to-Higgs closure is established.

- C2. Inclusive jets extension
- Motivation: jets can provide strong gluon leverage at overlapping and higher x, potentially strengthening the latent basis.
- Proposed observables:
- inclusive jet pT and rapidity spectra; optional dijet mass/chi distributions if theory/systematics are manageable.
- Scope choice:
- start with binned inclusive jets only; do not commit to unbinned jets in the proposal baseline.
- Scientific role:
- high-payoff auxiliary constraint channel if its theory covariance can be kept under control.
- Entry condition:
- require a demonstration that jet-scale and nonperturbative systematics do not dominate the latent fit.

- C3. SMEFT extension
- Motivation: once the Standard Model latent-PDF layer is established, ask whether one can disentangle proton-structure uncertainty from EFT-induced shape distortions in a joint interpretation.
- Minimal extension:
- introduce a small benchmark set of Wilson coefficients that affect top and Higgs differential spectra in correlated ways.
- Keep the first SMEFT study to a proof-of-principle with pseudo-data or simplified likelihoods.
- Scientific role:
- makes the program more original and future-facing, but must remain visibly downstream of the Standard Model validation.

8. Concrete synergy items

- Shared nuisance geometry
- build one latent nuisance basis used in both top and Higgs fits.
- Outcome: direct propagation of constrained uncertainty rather than ad hoc post-processing.

- Common observable map
- produce an x-Q2 overlap atlas showing which top bins constrain which Higgs bins.
- Outcome: a concrete visual and quantitative justification of the program.

- Channel-ranking logic
- decide whether single-top and jets are worth inclusion based on incremental information gain per added modeling risk.
- Outcome: a proposal that looks disciplined rather than additive.

- Combined publication path
- one methodological paper on latent PDF transfer,
- one CMS-facing analysis note or public-analysis style demonstration,
- optional third interpretation paper on SMEFT disentangling once baseline closure is proven.

9. Staged execution plan and milestones

- Stage 0: problem definition and scope freeze
- Deliverables:
- final baseline channels: ttbar -> differential Higgs(ggF),
- candidate extension channels: single-top, inclusive jets,
- baseline observable shortlist and theory setup.
- Validation:
- confirm overlapping x-Q2 coverage exists and is nontrivial.
- Risk:
- overlap may be weaker than expected for chosen observables.
- Why acceptable:
- even a limited overlap would clarify where transfer can and cannot work.
- Fallback:
- shift target Higgs observable set toward bins with stronger overlap, especially moderate pT(H) rather than only extreme tails.

- Stage 1: latent basis construction
- Deliverables:
- explicit latent parametrization,
- dimensionality choice,
- mapping from PDF variations to latent modes and to observable shifts.
- Validation:
- reconstruction test: a small number of latent modes must reproduce full PDF variation envelopes for the target observables within a predefined tolerance.
- Risk:
- the PDF dependence may not compress well into a low-dimensional basis.
- Why acceptable:
- this would itself be a useful negative result about the complexity of cross-channel transfer.
- Fallback:
- use a slightly larger but still structured nuisance basis, or split the basis by kinematic region.

- Stage 2: top-only constraint study
- Deliverables:
- top likelihood/profiling framework,
- ranking of top observables,
- posterior on latent modes from pseudo-data and, if feasible, real measurement inputs.
- Validation:
- closure on pseudo-data from held-out replicas,
- nuisance pull studies,
- sensitivity-to-binning tests.
- Risk:
- top observables may be more sensitive to scales/shower modeling than to the latent gluon modes.
- Why acceptable:
- it exposes where theory improvement is required before transfer claims are credible.
- Fallback:
- restrict to the most perturbatively stable observables or profile additional theory nuisances explicitly.

- Stage 3: propagation to Higgs
- Deliverables:
- propagated covariance/posterior for differential Higgs observables,
- uncertainty-reduction and correlation-change metrics,
- comparison of sequential versus simultaneous fit strategies.
- Validation:
- verify that posterior predictive intervals on Higgs close on pseudo-data generated from unseen PDF members.
- Risk:
- top constraints may not materially improve Higgs once realistic systematics are included.
- Why acceptable:
- the program still yields a sharp statement about transfer limits and where future data/theory effort should go.
- Fallback:
- redefine success to include improved uncertainty decomposition, cross-channel consistency diagnostics, or targeted gains in specific Higgs bins.

- Stage 4: robustness extensions
- Deliverables:
- controlled tests adding single-top and then inclusive jets,
- incremental information-gain analysis,
- recommendation on whether either channel belongs in the mature program.
- Validation:
- compare latent posterior shifts and predictive gains against added nuisance complexity.
- Risk:
- extra channels add more modeling noise than information.
- Why acceptable:
- this is exactly the decision the staged design is meant to expose.
- Fallback:
- keep them as robustness-only side studies rather than core ingredients.

- Stage 5: SMEFT proof-of-principle
- Deliverables:
- benchmark joint PDF+SMEFT inference study,
- diagnosis of degeneracies between latent proton-structure modes and EFT coefficients.
- Validation:
- injected-signal tests with pseudo-data,
- bias tests on Wilson-coefficient recovery with and without shared latent PDF control.
- Risk:
- EFT effects may be too degenerate with PDF/missing-higher-order effects for a clean first result.
- Why acceptable:
- identifying those degeneracies is itself scientifically useful and directly motivates future measurements.
- Fallback:
- narrow to one or two benchmark operators and one observable family, or publish as a methodological cautionary study.

10. Key observables and milestone-level metrics

- Top observables for baseline milestone
- m_tt differential spectrum
- pT(top) differential spectrum
- y_tt or y(top) to separate central from forward x-coverage
- optional double-differential m_tt vs y_tt if covariance and theory are under control

- Higgs observables for baseline milestone
- pT(H) in ggF-enhanced categories
- y_H differential spectrum
- N_jets and pT(H)+1j only if needed to expose transfer in radiation-sensitive regions

- Single-top extension observables
- t-channel top pT/y
- forward light-jet rapidity
- ST differential bins with strong acceptance control

- Jet extension observables
- inclusive jet pT in rapidity bins
- optional dijet mass only if the theory covariance model is already credible

- SMEFT benchmark observables
- high-pT Higgs tail
- high-m_tt or boosted-top tails
- one correlated top-Higgs shape pair for demonstrator fits

- Metrics for success
- compression fidelity of the latent basis
- posterior reduction in Higgs PDF uncertainty
- stability of gains under alternative theory nuisance models
- coverage/closure on held-out pseudo-data
- information gain from each extension channel relative to added nuisance dimension

11. Validation tests

- Pseudo-data closure
- generate pseudo-data from PDF replicas/eigenvectors not used to define the latent basis and verify unbiased recovery.

- Alternative generator/theory closure
- repeat core tests with alternative perturbative orders, scale choices, shower models, and heavy-quark settings where feasible.

- Observable-basis stability
- confirm that the inferred Higgs gain is not an artifact of one special top binning or one compressed variable set.

- Leave-one-observable-out tests
- remove one major top observable family at a time to identify whether the result is robust or driven by one fragile handle.

- Null transfer tests
- apply the learned latent constraint to observables with weak expected sensitivity to ensure the machinery is not manufacturing false precision.

- SMEFT bias tests
- inject EFT distortions and test whether the Standard Model latent fit misattributes them to proton structure; use this as the gatekeeper for any EFT claim.

12. Risk assessment and fallback logic

- Risk: latent basis is elegant but not sufficiently physical or portable
- Acceptable because the project would still produce a rigorous comparison between low-rank and full-nuisance treatments.
- Fallback: move to a semi-physical basis split by x/Q2 region or observable family.

- Risk: ttbar theory systematics dominate and wash out gluon information
- Acceptable because it pinpoints exactly what theory improvements or observable redesign are needed.
- Fallback: focus on perturbatively stable top observables and/or use normalized shapes where appropriate.

- Risk: Higgs gain is small once all uncertainties are included
- Acceptable because the cross-channel consistency map remains publishable and useful for future HL-LHC strategy.
- Fallback: sell the framework as uncertainty reallocation/consistency control rather than only precision gain.

- Risk: single-top adds electroweak and flavor-structure complications with limited gluon payoff
- Acceptable because it is explicitly a stage-4 extension, not a baseline dependency.
- Fallback: keep single-top as a stress test of transportability rather than a constraining input.

- Risk: inclusive jets offer strong nominal information but are hard to control theoretically and experimentally
- Acceptable because jets are the clearest high-risk, high-reward branch.
- Fallback: use jets only in restricted kinematic regions or omit them from the final combined constraint.

- Risk: SMEFT degeneracy makes the interpretation ambiguous
- Acceptable because exposing ambiguity is valuable and avoids overclaiming.
- Fallback: limit SMEFT to benchmark operators and pseudo-data studies until Standard Model control is demonstrably solid.

13. What makes the plan sparkling rather than generic

- It proposes a shared latent object, not just two neighboring measurements.
- It is staged so that ambitious branches are earned through validation rather than assumed.
- It is honest about where transfer should work, where extrapolation begins, and how failure would still teach something publishable.
- It creates a direct bridge from a top control program to a Higgs payoff, with visible decision points for single-top, jets, and SMEFT rather than bolting them on.
