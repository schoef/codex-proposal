1. Core thesis

Use CMS dileptonic ttbar data to constrain a fixed, low-dimensional latent description of the gluon PDF, then propagate that same latent description into one frozen first Higgs observable: the ggF Higgs rapidity spectrum in a clean fiducial measurement. The proposal is only credible if the latent directions are identifiable in top data under realistic top-modeling systematics and if those same directions have demonstrable overlap with the chosen Higgs target.

This plan therefore makes Milestone 1 a hard gate, not a soft exploratory phase. Before any Higgs-fit claim, the program must show that a pre-specified latent basis is distinguishable from top-modeling nuisance directions and that the fitted directions materially affect the chosen Higgs observable in an overlapping x,Q2 region.

2. Frozen first target and frozen latent basis

First Higgs target
- Freeze the first Higgs application to the single-differential ggF Higgs rapidity distribution, `d sigma / d |y_H|`, in a clean fiducial CMS measurement.
- Reason for this choice: Higgs rapidity is directly tied to the incoming parton x values, has a comparatively transparent PDF dependence, avoids making the first milestone hinge on Higgs recoil modeling, and is realistic for CMS differential measurements.
- Scope discipline: do not begin with inclusive Higgs combinations, double-differential `(y_H, pT_H)` targets, or broad “all differential Higgs” claims. Those are later extensions only if the first target passes the gate.

Pre-specified latent basis
- Freeze one latent gluon basis before fitting data. Use a 2-parameter basis built from standard PDF-eigenvector response projected onto the gluon-gluon parton luminosity in the overlap window relevant to dileptonic ttbar and ggF Higgs rapidity.
- Basis direction `g1`: leading normalization/tilt mode across the overlap region, roughly controlling the overall gluon luminosity around the x window most relevant for central Higgs rapidity and moderate-rapidity ttbar.
- Basis direction `g2`: leading shape/curvature mode, orthogonal to `g1`, capturing forward-versus-central migration in the same x window.
- Freeze the basis construction recipe up front:
- choose a fixed PDF prior family and eigenvector set
- define the overlap x-window once from truth-level support studies
- compute the response matrix of ttbar and Higgs observables to the PDF eigenvector variations
- orthogonalize to obtain the two latent directions
- lock the basis before any detector-level inference study
- Reason for freezing the basis: it prevents the proposal from “discovering” a basis that is accidentally aligned with top observables but irrelevant or unstable for Higgs transfer.

3. Main work packages

WP1. Overlap map and frozen basis construction
- Quantify the truth-level x,Q2 support of CMS dileptonic ttbar observables and the chosen ggF Higgs rapidity bins.
- Define one overlap window that is large enough to matter for both processes but narrow enough to avoid pretending that unrelated x regions are shared.
- Build the frozen 2-direction latent basis inside that window and publish the mapping from each basis direction to both top and Higgs observable shifts.
- Deliverable: a locked basis specification and a transfer map that is not allowed to change during Milestone 1.
- Main risk: the overlap window may already look weak or fragmented.
- Why acceptable: this is precisely the point where the proposal should fail early if the top-to-Higgs bridge is not physically well supported.
- Fallback: narrow the Higgs target further in rapidity or collapse to a 1-direction basis if only one stable overlap mode exists.

WP2. Detector-level ttbar inference with realistic nuisance structure
- Build the top-only inference in dileptonic ttbar using detector-level observables that are both experimentally credible and plausibly sensitive to the frozen latent basis.
- Candidate observables: lepton eta, dilepton rapidity, dilepton pT, mll, b-jet eta, jet multiplicity, HT, and event recoil variables.
- Include realistic nuisance directions from the start: detector response, backgrounds, scale variations, ISR/FSR, shower and hadronization model, color-reconnection-sensitive modeling, top pT modeling, and MC statistical uncertainty.
- The output is not just a posterior on `g1,g2`; it is a decomposition of whether information on `g1,g2` survives once the top-modeling nuisance space is turned on.
- Deliverable: profile/posterior constraints on the frozen basis and a nuisance-alignment diagnostic.
- Main risk: top-modeling variations span nearly the same space as the latent gluon directions.
- Why acceptable: exposing that degeneracy is central to the scientific honesty of the program.
- Fallback: restrict the observable set, add nuisance-orthogonal control summaries, or reduce the ambition to the single best-identified basis direction.

WP3. Hard-gated identifiability test
- Rewrite Milestone 1 as an identifiability test with two explicit failure modes:
- Failure mode A: top-modeling degeneracy. The frozen gluon basis cannot be separated from top-modeling nuisance directions in detector-level ttbar.
- Failure mode B: weak overlap. The basis directions constrained by ttbar have too little effect on `d sigma / d |y_H|` to justify transfer.
- The gate is passed only if both failure modes are defeated quantitatively.

Identifiability criteria against top-modeling degeneracy
- For each basis direction, measure its alignment with the span of top-modeling nuisance responses in the chosen observable space.
- Require a non-trivial orthogonal component of each retained basis direction outside the nuisance span.
- Require injected latent shifts to be recovered in closure tests without being absorbed predominantly by nuisance profiling.
- Require that the expected/posterior uncertainty reduction on at least one retained direction is driven by data information, not by prior choice.

Overlap criteria against weak transfer
- For each retained basis direction, compute the induced shift on the frozen Higgs rapidity spectrum and compare it to the expected Higgs statistical and systematic resolution.
- Require at least one retained direction to generate a stable, non-negligible effect on one or more Higgs rapidity bins after nuisance propagation.
- Run top-only fit plus Higgs prediction closure: inject a known latent shift, fit with top observables only, propagate to Higgs, and require coverage on the Higgs target.
- If the recovered Higgs effect is smaller than the Higgs measurement resolution or unstable under nuisance variations, the transfer claim fails.

Deliverable
- A decision table with only three allowed outcomes:
- Pass: at least one frozen basis direction is identifiable in ttbar and has robust overlap with Higgs rapidity.
- Partial pass: one direction is identifiable but the transfer effect is only large enough for a Higgs consistency test, not an uncertainty-reduction claim.
- Fail: identifiability is lost to nuisance degeneracy or overlap is too weak; the program stops or retargets before any Higgs propagation paper.

WP4. Sequential Higgs propagation, only after the gate
- Only if WP3 passes, propagate the top-constrained frozen basis into the ggF Higgs rapidity measurement as constrained nuisance parameters.
- Compare directly to the standard CMS treatment where PDF uncertainty is external.
- Produce three outputs:
- baseline Higgs uncertainty decomposition
- Higgs result with top-informed latent propagation
- consistency metric testing whether top-informed proton structure remains compatible with Higgs data
- Main risk: the gain in Higgs precision is modest.
- Why acceptable: after a successful gate, even a modest gain is scientifically meaningful because the transfer mechanism has been validated rather than assumed.
- Fallback: if only the partial-pass condition is met, frame this stage as a proton-structure consistency test instead of a precision-improvement result.

WP5. Later extensions, only if the first target works
- Extend from Higgs rapidity to a coarse `(y_H, pT_H)` measurement only if the first target passes cleanly.
- Consider a simultaneous top-plus-Higgs likelihood only after the sequential path has already produced a stable result.
- Consider additional channels only after the first target and first basis are demonstrably useful.
- Main risk: scope creep weakens the central claim.
- Why acceptable: the plan is explicitly staged to block premature expansion.
- Fallback: enforce a rule that no new channel or basis is added before the first target is complete.

4. Milestone 1 as the decisive gate

Milestone 1
- Process side: CMS dileptonic ttbar only
- Frozen Higgs-facing target: ggF Higgs rapidity spectrum
- Frozen latent object: 2-direction gluon basis `g1,g2`
- Objective: test identifiability of `g1,g2` in top data under realistic nuisance structure and test whether at least one retained direction has robust overlap with the Higgs target

Milestone 1 pass conditions
- The basis is frozen before detector-level fitting and not redesigned after seeing which directions are easiest to measure.
- At least one basis direction is constrained by top data beyond prior information under realistic nuisance profiling.
- That retained direction is not nearly collinear with the dominant top-modeling nuisance span in the detector-level response space.
- Injected shifts in the retained direction are recovered in top closure tests with acceptable bias.
- The retained direction produces a stable and non-negligible shift in the frozen Higgs rapidity observable after full nuisance propagation.
- Top-only fit to injected shifts yields propagated Higgs predictions with acceptable coverage.

Milestone 1 failure conditions
- The apparent top sensitivity to `g1,g2` disappears once realistic ISR/FSR, shower, scale, or other top-modeling nuisances are profiled.
- The recovered latent shifts depend strongly on basis redefinition, prior choice, or analysis compression.
- The retained directions have too little effect on Higgs rapidity to matter for the intended transfer.
- Closure succeeds in top but fails after propagation to Higgs.

Interpretation of failure
- Failure is scientifically useful because it establishes that the naive top-to-Higgs PDF bridge does not survive realistic CMS conditions for this target and basis.
- A fail outcome still yields a publishable statement about where internal proton-structure calibration breaks down: nuisance degeneracy, weak overlap, or both.

5. Staged plan

Near-term: 0-9 months
- Freeze the first Higgs target and frozen basis definition.
- Build truth-level x,Q2 overlap diagnostics and basis response maps.
- Implement detector-level ttbar inference with the full nuisance menu needed for the identifiability gate.
- Run injected-signal closure and nuisance-alignment studies.

Mid-term: 9-18 months
- Complete the hard-gated Milestone 1 decision.
- If passed, implement sequential propagation into the Higgs rapidity measurement.
- If only partially passed, implement a Higgs consistency test without claiming strong uncertainty reduction.
- If failed, stop expansion and document the failure mode sharply.

Later-stage: 18+ months
- If the first target works, broaden to coarse `(y_H, pT_H)` or a simultaneous fit.
- Reuse the validated basis and gate logic before adding any new observable or channel.

6. Risk assessment and fallback logic

Risk 1. Top-modeling degeneracy dominates the latent basis
- Problem: ISR/FSR, shower, scale, or hadronization responses mimic `g1,g2` so well that identifiability is illusory.
- Why acceptable: this is the central adversarial test the proposal should force itself to face.
- Fallback: keep only the single best-separated direction or end the program at a top-only negative result.

Risk 2. Weak x-overlap makes Higgs transfer too small to matter
- Problem: the frozen basis is measurable in ttbar but has too small or too unstable an effect on Higgs rapidity.
- Why acceptable: this tells us the top information is not useful for this Higgs target, which is a real physics conclusion.
- Fallback: report a no-transfer result or retarget to a narrower Higgs rapidity region only if done as a new stage after failure is documented.

Risk 3. Basis instability across theory setups
- Problem: the chosen latent basis is not stable under reasonable PDF prior or generator choices.
- Why acceptable: a basis that is not stable should not be used for transfer claims.
- Fallback: collapse to a more conservative 1-direction basis tied directly to standard PDF response.

Risk 4. Higgs-side gain is real but small
- Problem: even after a passed gate, the practical Higgs uncertainty reduction may be modest.
- Why acceptable: a validated shared latent proton-structure inference is already scientifically differentiated from standard external-PDF treatment.
- Fallback: emphasize the consistency-test result and defer precision-gain claims to larger datasets or combinations.

Risk 5. Scope drift weakens the proposal
- Problem: adding more targets before the first one is validated will blur the scientific claim.
- Why acceptable: the plan already contains a hard stop after Milestone 1.
- Fallback: enforce that all expansion proposals must reuse the same gate logic and cannot begin until the first target is resolved.

7. What would count as success

Minimum success
- One frozen latent gluon direction is identifiable in realistic dileptonic ttbar data.
- That same direction has robust overlap with ggF Higgs rapidity and survives propagation closure.
- CMS can therefore make a defensible statement that part of the Higgs PDF uncertainty is internally testable with top data for this specific target.

Strong success
- Sequential propagation into the Higgs rapidity measurement yields either a measurable uncertainty reduction or a sharp top-to-Higgs proton-structure consistency validation that is clearly distinct from the standard external-PDF treatment.

High-payoff success
- The hard-gated logic scales to a broader Higgs target or a simultaneous fit without losing identifiability, turning proton structure into a shared latent CMS inference object rather than a purely external nuisance input.

8. Why this version is sharper

The proposal is now narrower and harder to fool. It fixes one first Higgs target, one latent basis, and one adversarial gate before the expensive Higgs integration work begins. That improves coherence, makes failure informative, and turns Milestone 1 into a scientifically meaningful test rather than a vague “learn some latent directions” phase.
