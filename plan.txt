1. Core thesis

Use CMS dileptonic ttbar data as an in-situ constraint on a small set of gluon-sensitive latent directions, then propagate those same directions into a differential gluon-fusion Higgs measurement. The scientific claim is not merely that top improves Higgs indirectly, but that CMS can test whether one common proton-structure description consistently explains both processes in the overlapping x and Q2 region.

The plan should be staged so that the first result is narrow, publishable, and technically credible on its own: a top-only CMS analysis that identifies and validates detector-level gluon-sensitive latent directions and quantifies how much information they carry for a pre-defined Higgs phase-space target. The Higgs-facing part enters immediately through transfer tests, but the first milestone does not require a full joint top-plus-Higgs likelihood.

2. Main work packages

WP1. Define a compact proton-structure parameterization for CMS use
- Replace a full PDF functional fit with 2-5 latent gluon directions chosen to span the x,Q2 region relevant to dileptonic ttbar and differential ggF Higgs production.
- Build these directions from PDF eigenvector response, score compression, or another locally linear basis that is interpretable and easy to propagate through CMS templates or simulation-based inference.
- Require from the start that each latent direction has a clear kinematic meaning: which x range it probes, which observables constrain it, and which Higgs bins it affects.
- Output: a CMS-usable latent gluon basis with a transfer map from top-sensitive observables to Higgs-sensitive observables.
- Main risk: the basis may be too crude to capture relevant deformations.
- Why acceptable: even failure is informative because it identifies which regions require higher-dimensional or process-specific treatment.
- Fallback: expand the basis modestly or restrict the scope to one dominant direction tied to the Higgs observable chosen for milestone 2.

WP2. Top-only detector-level inference in dileptonic ttbar
- Build an unbinned or weakly compressed inference strategy for CMS dileptonic ttbar events using observables that are realistic at detector level and sensitive to gluon kinematics.
- Candidate observables: lepton eta, dilepton rapidity, dilepton pT, mll, jet multiplicity, b-jet eta, HT, and event-level recoil observables that correlate with partonic x and radiation pattern.
- Include realistic nuisance handling from the beginning: detector response, background normalization, top modeling, ISR/FSR, shower/hadronization, scale variations, and MC statistical limitations.
- Validate by closure tests, nuisance-stress tests, and ranking of which observables constrain which latent gluon directions.
- Output: posterior or profile constraints on the latent gluon parameters from top data alone, plus an uncertainty decomposition that isolates modeling limits versus statistical power.
- Main risk: top-modeling systematics may dominate and wash out the PDF information.
- Why acceptable: that still establishes the practical boundary between conceptual possibility and CMS-realistic sensitivity.
- Fallback: restrict to a smaller observable set with better modeling control, use stronger summary-statistic compression, or target a reduced fiducial region with cleaner transfer properties.

WP3. Explicit top-to-Higgs transfer tests
- Before claiming any Higgs uncertainty reduction, perform dedicated transfer tests that ask whether the latent gluon directions constrained in top predict Higgs response stably and non-trivially.
- Test A: sensitivity-overlap test. For each latent direction, compare the top Fisher information and the Higgs response in candidate Higgs bins; retain only bins with genuine shared sensitivity.
- Test B: closure-under-shift test. Inject known latent gluon deformations in simulation, fit them using top observables only, then propagate to Higgs and check whether the Higgs prediction is recovered within uncertainties.
- Test C: nuisance-robustness test. Repeat transfer under top-modeling and Higgs-modeling deformations to identify whether apparent transfer is actually absorbed by unrelated nuisances.
- Test D: basis-stability test. Verify that transfer conclusions are stable under reasonable changes of latent basis construction and event compression.
- Output: a quantitative decision table stating which Higgs observables are defensible transfer targets, which latent directions transfer reliably, and where the program should not over-claim.
- Main risk: the overlap may be weak once realistic nuisances are included.
- Why acceptable: this is exactly the critical scientific question, and a negative answer still sharpens future CMS strategy by identifying non-transferable regions.
- Fallback: narrow the Higgs target further, keep the result as a top-constrained prior study, or reformulate the first Higgs application as a consistency test rather than an uncertainty-reduction claim.

WP4. Narrow first Higgs milestone: one differential ggF measurement with propagated top constraint
- Choose one CMS-realistic Higgs observable with clear gluon sensitivity and manageable analysis complexity. Preferred target: a low-dimensional differential ggF measurement such as Higgs rapidity, or a coarse double-differential measurement in Higgs rapidity and low-to-moderate Higgs pT if the transfer tests justify it.
- Start from a sequential architecture: infer latent gluon constraints from top, pass them into the Higgs likelihood as constrained nuisance parameters, and compare against the conventional external-PDF treatment.
- Keep the first milestone narrow by limiting both the number of Higgs bins and the dimensionality of the latent basis. The milestone should answer one clean question: does CMS top data measurably constrain the PDF component of this Higgs measurement in the matched phase-space region?
- Deliver side-by-side results:
- baseline Higgs uncertainty with standard PDF treatment
- Higgs uncertainty after top-informed latent propagation
- consistency metric showing whether the top-informed posterior remains compatible with the Higgs data
- Output: a concrete CMS-style differential Higgs result with internalized proton-structure information, even if the effect is modest.
- Main risk: Higgs statistics and systematics may make the gain too small for a compelling standalone claim.
- Why acceptable: the transfer-consistency result is still scientifically meaningful even with little uncertainty reduction.
- Fallback: frame the first Higgs result explicitly as a validation of internal proton-structure propagation and move the uncertainty-reduction claim to a later luminosity or combination stage.

WP5. Later-stage simultaneous top-plus-Higgs fit
- If the sequential program succeeds, promote the analysis to a simultaneous likelihood with shared latent gluon parameters and correlated nuisance treatment where justified.
- Use the simultaneous stage to test whether top and Higgs jointly prefer a common proton-structure description and whether any tension appears once both sectors are fitted together.
- Treat this as a second-wave objective, not a prerequisite for the first paper.
- Output: a combined CMS measurement concept in which proton structure is a shared latent component across flagship analyses.
- Main risk: technical complexity may outrun the physics gain.
- Why acceptable: by this stage the sequential path has already produced publishable physics and diagnostic evidence.
- Fallback: keep the program sequential and reserve simultaneity for a methodological extension or HL-LHC roadmap.

3. Narrow first milestone

Milestone 1 should be intentionally small and decisive:
- Process: CMS dileptonic ttbar only
- Goal: extract 1-2 leading gluon-sensitive latent directions at detector level
- Scope: realistic nuisance model, closure tests, and explicit transfer forecasts to one pre-selected Higgs observable
- Required deliverable: a validated statement of whether CMS top data provide a stable and useful constraint on the proton-structure directions relevant to that Higgs observable

Why this is the right first milestone:
- It is publishable even without a Higgs fit.
- It forces the proposal to identify the actual transferable information rather than assume it.
- It creates a hard gate before the more expensive Higgs integration work.

Concrete success criteria for Milestone 1:
- at least one latent direction is constrained by top data beyond prior information under realistic nuisances
- that direction has a demonstrable, non-negligible effect on the chosen Higgs observable
- transfer closure succeeds under injected shifts in simulation
- modeling systematics do not fully destroy the transfer claim in the retained phase-space region

4. Explicit top-to-Higgs transfer strategy

The proposal should state clearly that transfer is not assumed; it is tested.

Transfer target selection:
- Prefer Higgs bins whose partonic x support overlaps materially with the top-constrained region.
- Start with observables where the mapping from PDF deformation to rate or shape is simple enough to interpret.
- Avoid broad inclusive Higgs claims in the first phase.

Transfer metrics:
- overlap in x,Q2 support between top-sensitive events and target Higgs bins
- correlation or information transfer coefficient between latent directions inferred in top and Higgs prediction shifts
- posterior predictive coverage when top-constrained latent parameters are propagated to Higgs pseudo-data
- stability of the transfer result under nuisance variations and basis changes

Decision logic:
- If overlap and closure are strong, proceed to sequential Higgs propagation.
- If overlap is moderate but robust, proceed with a Higgs consistency test rather than a precision-gain claim.
- If overlap is weak or unstable, keep the result top-only and retarget the Higgs observable or latent basis.

5. Staged execution plan

Near-term: 0-12 months
- Define the latent gluon basis and the x,Q2 overlap map between dileptonic ttbar and candidate ggF Higgs observables.
- Build the top-only detector-level inference setup with a limited but realistic nuisance model.
- Run closure and ranking studies to identify the dominant top observables and the leading transferable latent directions.
- Complete the explicit top-to-Higgs transfer tests for one Higgs target observable.

Mid-term: 12-24 months
- Freeze the first Higgs target and implement the sequential propagated-constraint analysis.
- Compare top-informed Higgs results against the conventional external-PDF treatment.
- Expand nuisance realism and validate robustness with alternative generators, shower models, and analysis compressions.
- Decide, based on measured transfer strength, whether to broaden the Higgs binning or keep the scope narrow.

Later-stage: 24+ months
- Build the simultaneous top-plus-Higgs likelihood if the sequential program showed real transfer.
- Explore whether additional channels or observables strengthen the common proton-structure picture.
- Possible extensions, only after the core program works: more differential Higgs measurements, additional top channels, limited SMEFT-PDF interplay studies.

6. Risk assessment and fallback logic

Risk 1. Transfer does not survive realistic systematics
- Problem: the latent directions learned in top may not predict Higgs observables robustly once modeling uncertainties are included.
- Why acceptable: disproving naive transfer is a real scientific result and defines the boundary of CMS-internal proton calibration.
- Fallback: recast the output as a transfer-consistency study, reduce the Higgs phase space, or stop after Milestone 1 with a strong top-only conclusion.

Risk 2. Top-modeling nuisance directions align with PDF directions
- Problem: ISR/FSR, scales, and shower effects may mimic the same shape changes as gluon deformations.
- Why acceptable: disentangling this is central to the proposal’s credibility.
- Fallback: redesign the observable set for orthogonality, add control observables, or restrict to the best-separated latent direction rather than overfitting several.

Risk 3. Higgs side is too statistically sparse for unbinned or flexible inference
- Problem: the Higgs measurement may not support the same inference machinery as top.
- Why acceptable: the scientific innovation is the shared latent description, not identical statistical treatment on both sides.
- Fallback: keep the top side unbinned and use a binned or compressed Higgs likelihood.

Risk 4. The latent basis is not stable across generators or theory setups
- Problem: an apparently informative basis may depend too strongly on how it was constructed.
- Why acceptable: basis dependence is measurable and can be included in the method validation.
- Fallback: use a more conservative basis tied to standard PDF eigenvector responses and limit claims to basis-stable directions.

Risk 5. Scope creep dilutes the proposal
- Problem: adding more channels too early will weaken the central argument.
- Why acceptable: the staged structure prevents this if enforced.
- Fallback: formal rule that no extra channel is added before Milestone 1 succeeds and the first Higgs target is demonstrated.

7. What would count as success

Minimum success
- CMS top data constrain at least one interpretable gluon-sensitive latent direction at detector level.
- That direction passes explicit transfer tests to one chosen ggF Higgs observable.
- The propagated constraint yields either a measurable Higgs PDF uncertainty reduction or a non-trivial top-to-Higgs consistency validation.

Strong success
- A sequential CMS Higgs measurement with top-informed latent proton constraints is shown to be both robust and meaningfully different from the standard external-PDF treatment.
- The resulting uncertainty model is clearer and more physically grounded than a purely imported PDF nuisance prescription.

High-payoff success
- A simultaneous top-plus-Higgs fit demonstrates that a shared latent proton-structure description can be sustained across both sectors, turning PDFs from an external ingredient into a partially internal CMS inference target.

8. Why this plan is scientifically differentiated

The distinguishing feature is not just better use of machine learning or more differential data. It is the introduction of a testable shared latent proton-structure layer connecting top and Higgs measurements inside one CMS program. The narrow first milestone makes the proposal credible, while the explicit transfer tests prevent over-claiming. If the program works, it changes how PDF uncertainty enters Higgs precision measurements; if it fails, it still produces a sharp statement about where internal proton calibration does and does not work at CMS.
