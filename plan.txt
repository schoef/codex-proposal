1. Physics case: the proposal is to turn `ttbar` production into an in-situ calibration of the gluon PDF at the LHC, and then use that calibration directly in gluon-fusion Higgs measurements. The central claim is not simply that unbinned methods are more powerful, but that detector-level `ttbar` events carry enough differential information to determine the gluon combination that matters for `gg -> H`, thereby reducing the usual reliance on an external global PDF fit.
2. Measurement concept: perform a joint detector-level likelihood fit to `ttbar` and fiducial `ggH -> gamma gamma` events, using one common latent parameterization of the gluon PDF. `ttbar` is the anchor channel because it is abundant, predominantly gluon-initiated at the LHC, and probes exactly the `x` range that overlaps with inclusive and differential Higgs production. The Higgs sample then plays two roles: first as a transfer test of the `ttbar`-derived gluon information, and second, only if justified by the data and nuisance structure, as a possible additional constraint.
3. PDF strategy: define the gluon freedom once from a top-only study of `delta ln g(x,Q0)` at `Q0 = m_t`, keeping only the best-measured directions in the `ttbar` likelihood. These modes are then evolved with DGLAP to the Higgs scale without redefining the basis. In HEP terms, this is a controlled reduced-parameter PDF fit tuned to the kinematic support of the analysis, rather than a full global-fit replacement.
4. Physics target for the Higgs program: pre-register a single Higgs-relevant gluon quantity, `Phi_ggH = int dx w_ggH(x) delta ln g(x,Q = m_H)`, where `w_ggH(x)` is the gluon-gluon luminosity weight for fiducial `ggH -> gamma gamma`. This should be presented as the effective gluon direction controlling the Higgs acceptance-level rate in the chosen fiducial setup. All claims about improvement, robustness, and Higgs sensitivity should be reduced to this one target quantity, so that the analysis does not drift into ambiguous measures of "better PDFs".
5. Event information retained in the fit: on the `ttbar` side, the point is to keep the full reconstructed detector-level event information available to the unbinned likelihood surrogate, rather than compressing the measurement into a few unfolded spectra. On the Higgs side, keep the observable choice intentionally conservative: `m_gammagamma` to isolate signal from continuum background, `|y_H|` as the cleanest handle on the Bjorken-`x` values of the incoming gluons, and `pT_H` only as a conditional extra observable whose usefulness must be demonstrated after nuisance profiling.
6. Primary performance metric: compare the unbinned strategy to a matched binned baseline through `Gain(Phi_ggH) = Var_binned(Phi_ggH | full profiling) / Var_method(Phi_ggH | full profiling)`. This is the proposal’s key quantitative statement: after profiling experimental and theory systematics, by how much does the detector-level unbinned treatment improve the uncertainty on the gluon combination relevant for `ggH`?
7. Stage 1, `ttbar` closure and basis definition: start with a `ttbar`-only analysis and determine the smallest number of retained gluon modes `k` that captures at least `95%` of the top-only Fisher information in `x in [8e-3, 8e-2]`, which is the overlap region most relevant for the combined `ttbar`-Higgs program. Require pseudo-data closure at the level of coefficient recovery within `0.3 sigma`, 68% interval coverage in `[60%, 76%]`, and at least a `10%` gain on `Phi_ggH` relative to the matched binned baseline. If this stage fails, there is no case for moving to Higgs.
8. Stage 2, Higgs transfer measurement: add fiducial `ggH -> gamma gamma` information using only `(m_gammagamma, |y_H|)`, with the same PDF basis, nuisance model, and priors. This is the cleanest HEP interpretation of the Higgs addition, because `|y_H|` maps directly onto the initial-state momentum fractions while avoiding an early over-commitment to more modelling-sensitive observables. If the combined fit does not improve `Phi_ggH` by at least `10%`, the correct conclusion is that Higgs acts as a non-trivial validation of the `ttbar`-derived gluon calibration, but not as a new proton-structure constraint.
9. Stage 3, test whether differential Higgs information survives profiling: extend the Higgs fit to `(m_gammagamma, |y_H|, pT_H)`. In physics terms, this asks whether the extra radiation pattern encoded in `pT_H` contains stable additional sensitivity to the same gluon direction, once background modelling, acceptance effects, recoil modelling, and theory nuisances are allowed to float. A genuine Higgs-added constraint should be claimed only if adding `pT_H` improves the uncertainty on `Phi_ggH` by at least `15%` relative to the `|y_H|`-only Higgs setup under identical profiling assumptions.
10. Systematics and robustness program: repeat the nominal fit in three configurations, nuisances fixed/PDF floating, PDF fixed/nuisances floating, and both floating, to separate pure information gain from nuisance reabsorption. Then stress the Higgs side by widening shape priors and injecting mismodelling in recoil tails and continuum background shapes. If the gain in `Phi_ggH` disappears, the bias exceeds `0.3 sigma`, or coverage breaks down, the Higgs component should be explicitly reclassified as modelling-sensitive. That is a valid outcome and should be reported as such.
11. Adjacent measurements that genuinely belong to this program: the nearest extensions are processes that probe the same gluon sector without turning the proposal into a mini-global fit. The first is inclusive or differential `tW` / single-top associated production, which is less directly gluon-dominated than `ttbar` but useful as a cross-check on flavour assumptions and on the interplay between gluon and quark directions. The second is `Z + jet` or high-`pT` photon production, not as a core constraint, but as a control handle on jet/photon calibration and recoil systematics that otherwise contaminate Higgs differential information. A third possible extension is inclusive jet production, but this should be treated cautiously: it is powerful for gluons, yet theory and non-perturbative systematics are sufficiently different that it would quickly drag the analysis toward a true global-fit environment. Drell-Yan by itself is not a primary PDF lever for the gluon direction of interest, but it remains useful for detector calibration, lepton efficiencies, and luminosity-related cross-checks. The right philosophy is therefore: keep `ttbar` plus `ggH` as the irreducible core, use `Z/gamma + jet` and DY only as control or validation channels, and introduce single-top or jets only if a later stage shows a clearly identified degeneracy that they can break.
12. Physics payoff, risk, and fallback: the ambitious outcome is a fully detector-level statement that top data alone can calibrate the gluon direction governing gluon-fusion Higgs production, with Higgs data either confirming that transfer or sharpening it. The main risk is that `pT_H` and adjacent channels do not add stable PDF information after realistic profiling. That is still a useful physics result: it would delimit where the information really comes from, support a cleaner `ttbar`-anchored interpretation, and show that the strongest and most robust statement is an internal LHC calibration of the Higgs-relevant gluon luminosity from top-pair events rather than from a broad external PDF machinery.
