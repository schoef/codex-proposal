1. Core thesis

- Minimal publishable baseline: recast the program as an identifiability test, not a broad analysis platform.
- Main question: can one carefully chosen `ttbar` observable family identify a low-dimensional, physically transferable gluon-PDF subspace that explains a measurable fraction of PDF-induced variance in one or two `ggF` Higgs observables?
- Scientific claim to test: some Higgs PDF uncertainty is not generic "theory noise" but lives in a common proton-structure subspace that can be constrained in situ by top data.
- Scope discipline:
- One `ttbar` observable family only: baseline choice `m_tt` differential spectrum, optionally with a central/forward split through `|y_tt|`.
- One or two `ggF` Higgs observables only: baseline choice `pT(H)` and, if needed for a second projection, `|y_H|`.
- One transferable PDF subspace only: a rank-1 baseline, with rank-2 allowed only if rank-1 demonstrably fails but the second mode is interpretable.
- No single-top, inclusive jets, or SMEFT in the baseline paper. They remain future branches motivated only after the identifiability test is answered.

2. Why this narrow core belongs together

- `ttbar` and `ggF` Higgs are both dominantly gluon initiated at the LHC, but probe different hard scales and final states. That is exactly why transfer is nontrivial and publishable.
- `m_tt` is the cleanest single `ttbar` family for a first test because it has direct sensitivity to gluon luminosity across a controlled `x` range and a clear interpretation in terms of partonic kinematics.
- `pT(H)` is the best primary Higgs target because it is experimentally relevant, theory-sensitive, and exposes whether the learned PDF direction transfers beyond inclusive rate information.
- `|y_H|` is the natural optional second Higgs observable because it is more directly tied to parton-luminosity structure and can separate "transfer in normalization-like directions" from "transfer in radiation-sensitive shapes".

3. Precise baseline hypothesis

- Hypothesis H1: the PDF covariance of the chosen Higgs observable set contains a dominant component aligned with a transferable gluon-PDF subspace inferred from `ttbar`.
- Null hypothesis H0: after realistic nuisance treatment, `ttbar` does not identify any subspace that robustly predicts Higgs PDF variation beyond weak or accidental correlations.
- Publishable output either way:
- Positive result: a concrete demonstration that CMS top data can constrain a physically meaningful part of Higgs PDF uncertainty.
- Negative result: a sharp boundary on how much Higgs PDF variance is actually transferable from top once nuisance degeneracies are included.

4. Baseline observable design

- `ttbar` control family
- Primary observable: normalized and absolute `m_tt` spectrum.
- Minimal variant set: inclusive `m_tt`; optional split into central and forward `|y_tt|` regions if needed to improve `x` localization without broadening the scope too much.
- Reason for this choice: it isolates one coherent physics family instead of mixing many top observables with overlapping nuisance structure.

- `ggF` Higgs target family
- Primary target: differential `pT(H)`.
- Secondary target only if justified: `|y_H|`.
- Reason for this pairing:
- `pT(H)` tests whether transfer survives in a practically important differential observable.
- `|y_H|` tests a more luminosity-driven structure and helps diagnose whether failure in `pT(H)` comes from QCD-radiation modeling rather than absence of a shared PDF direction.

- Observable-count discipline
- The baseline should remain publishable with `m_tt -> pT(H)` alone.
- `|y_H|` enters only if it materially sharpens the identifiability argument.

5. Physical transferable PDF subspace

- Parameterize the transferable uncertainty as a low-rank subspace in PDF-variation space projected onto the chosen observables.
- Baseline construction:
- Start from PDF replicas or Hessian eigenvector responses for `m_tt`, `pT(H)`, and optionally `|y_H|`.
- Extract the leading common mode between `ttbar` and Higgs observable responses by SVD / canonical-correlation-style compression in observable space.
- Require the mode to be physically interpretable as a gluon-luminosity direction over a defined `x-Q^2` support, not merely a numerical fit artifact.

- Rank choice
- Rank-1 is the default and preferred publishable baseline.
- Escalate to rank-2 only if:
- rank-1 misses a large fraction of Higgs PDF variance,
- and the second mode is stable across theory settings and not dominated by nuisance contamination.

- Why this is the right abstraction
- It is smaller and more testable than a generic latent-space program.
- It matches the professor's requirement to specify one physical transferable PDF subspace.

6. Identifiability test

- Core identifiability question:
- Is the transferable PDF mode learnable from `ttbar` well enough that its posterior uncertainty materially contracts the same mode in Higgs predictions?

- Operational test
- Step A: build the candidate common mode from theory responses alone.
- Step B: fit or profile that mode using `ttbar` pseudo-data and realistic nuisance parameters.
- Step C: propagate the posterior on that mode into Higgs predictions.
- Step D: measure how much Higgs PDF variance along the pre-identified common mode is captured and how robust that result is to nuisance freedom.

- Identifiability criteria
- Criterion 1: mode stability.
- The extracted common mode should be stable under reasonable changes in perturbative settings, binning choices, and PDF set representation.
- Criterion 2: `ttbar` learnability.
- The posterior uncertainty of the mode from `ttbar` should shrink relative to the prior by a clearly nontrivial amount.
- Criterion 3: Higgs relevance.
- The mode should account for a significant fraction of the prior PDF variance in `pT(H)` and optionally `|y_H|`.
- Criterion 4: nuisance robustness.
- The apparent Higgs gain should survive profiling of main experimental/theory nuisance directions rather than disappearing once nuisance freedom is restored.

7. Explicit success and failure criteria

- Success criterion S1: captured Higgs variance.
- Rank-1 mode captures at least `30-50%` of the prior PDF variance in at least one Higgs observable family over a defined bin range.
- More ambitious success: rank-1 captures `>50%` for `|y_H|` and a nontrivial `>25-30%` for `pT(H)`.
- If the exact percentage depends strongly on the chosen bins, the proposal should predefine a fiducial range and report integrated and bin-wise fractions.

- Success criterion S2: posterior impact from `ttbar`.
- `ttbar` constraint reduces the uncertainty of the transferable mode by enough to yield a visible Higgs uncertainty reduction, e.g. `>=10-20%` in the affected Higgs variance component or a statistically clear contraction in posterior predictive bands.

- Success criterion S3: nuisance robustness.
- The transferred gain should remain after profiling major nuisance classes:
- scale variations,
- parton shower / hadronization model differences,
- top-mass dependence if relevant,
- detector/unfolding nuisance model for the `ttbar` input.
- A practical threshold: at least half of the nominal Higgs-variance gain seen in the PDF-only closure test survives in the full nuisance model.

- Failure criterion F1: low Higgs alignment.
- The leading common mode inferred from `ttbar` explains less than about `20%` of Higgs PDF variance across all reasonable observable choices.
- Interpretation: the shared subspace is too weak for a useful baseline transfer claim.

- Failure criterion F2: nuisance fragility.
- The apparent common mode is present in PDF-only studies but collapses once theory/experimental nuisances are profiled.
- Interpretation: the transfer is not identifiable in a realistic analysis environment.

- Failure criterion F3: unstable mode definition.
- The rank-1 mode changes qualitatively with minor theory/basis choices.
- Interpretation: the proposal has not isolated a physical transferable direction.

- Why failure is still informative
- It would set a concrete limit on cross-process PDF transferability and show that broader "top constrains Higgs PDFs" claims need stronger channels or better theory control.

8. Staged execution plan

- Stage 0: scope freeze and kinematic justification
- Fix the baseline to `m_tt -> pT(H)` with optional `|y_H|`.
- Produce an `x-Q^2` overlap map for the chosen bins.
- Decide whether a central/forward `|y_tt|` split is necessary.
- Deliverable: a one-page baseline definition with observables, bin ranges, perturbative settings, and PDF inputs.
- Main risk: the chosen `m_tt` bins overlap poorly with the Higgs target region.
- Why acceptable: this is quick to test and may itself motivate the rapidity split.
- Fallback: keep the same `ttbar` family but add the minimal `|y_tt|` split rather than adding new observables.

- Stage 1: construct the transferable PDF mode
- Compute response vectors of the selected observables to PDF variations.
- Extract the leading common mode and quantify how much Higgs variance it spans before any fit.
- Deliverable: one explicit candidate transferable mode with a variance-capture table.
- Main risk: the common structure is spread over many weak directions.
- Why acceptable: it directly tests whether the latent-space idea is overclaimed.
- Fallback: allow rank-2, but only if both modes remain interpretable and the analysis still reads as a minimal identifiability test.

- Stage 2: `ttbar`-only inference
- Build a likelihood for the `m_tt` family with the transferable mode and a controlled nuisance model.
- Perform closure tests on pseudo-data generated from held-out PDF members / replicas.
- Deliverable: posterior on the transferable mode and a learnability metric.
- Main risk: `m_tt` sensitivity is swamped by scale or shower nuisance directions.
- Why acceptable: that is exactly the identifiability barrier the paper should measure.
- Fallback: restrict to the most stable `m_tt` bins and report the result as a phase-space-limited identifiability study.

- Stage 3: propagate to Higgs and evaluate captured variance
- Propagate the fitted mode posterior into `pT(H)` and optionally `|y_H|`.
- Quantify:
- fraction of Higgs PDF variance aligned with the transferable mode,
- posterior reduction of that aligned variance,
- total Higgs uncertainty reduction if reported.
- Deliverable: the central identifiability result with explicit success/failure verdict.
- Main risk: the mode is real but too weak to produce visible Higgs improvement.
- Why acceptable: the paper still establishes a measurable upper bound on transferable information.
- Fallback: emphasize mode-alignment and posterior-predictive consistency rather than raw uncertainty reduction.

- Stage 4: robustness audit
- Repeat the propagation with expanded nuisance treatments and minor theory variations.
- Deliverable: a nuisance-robustness table stating what fraction of the nominal gain survives.
- Main risk: robustness checks erase the effect.
- Why acceptable: that would be the scientifically honest endpoint of the baseline study.
- Fallback: publish as a "limits of transferability" result with recommendations for what extra theory control or observables would be required.

9. Validation logic

- PDF-only closure
- Generate pseudo-data from held-out PDF members.
- Check whether the transferable mode recovered from `ttbar` predicts the held-out Higgs shift.

- Nuisance-augmented closure
- Inject simultaneous PDF and nuisance shifts.
- Check whether the fitted transferable mode remains unbiased and whether the Higgs posterior remains calibrated.

- Basis stability checks
- Compare rank-1 mode extraction across:
- Hessian vs replica representation if available,
- modest theory-scale choices,
- binning coarseness variations.
- The mode need not be numerically identical, but its Higgs variance-capture and physical interpretation should remain stable.

- Null checks
- Test an intentionally weakly aligned observable or bin subset.
- Confirm that the method does not claim spurious transfer where none should exist.

10. Key dependencies

- Theory predictions for `ttbar` and `ggF` Higgs with consistent PDF variations and nuisance bookkeeping.
- A fast response infrastructure for projecting PDF variations into observable space.
- Access to realistic covariance / nuisance models for the chosen `ttbar` measurement family and Higgs prediction setup.
- A disciplined definition of the fiducial Higgs bin range; otherwise variance-capture claims will be too easy to game.

11. Risk assessment and fallback summary

- Risk: no dominant transferable subspace exists.
- Why acceptable: disproving the existence of a simple shared mode is already a publishable clarification.
- Fallback: report a quantitative upper bound on low-rank transferability and motivate why broader channels would be needed.

- Risk: one common mode exists in theory but is not identifiable after nuisances.
- Why acceptable: this distinguishes correlation from useful constraint, which is a strong scientific message.
- Fallback: narrow the claim to PDF-only geometry plus an explicit statement that realistic nuisance control is the bottleneck.

- Risk: `pT(H)` is too contaminated by radiation-model dependence to serve as the cleanest first target.
- Why acceptable: this is why `|y_H|` is retained as the only optional second Higgs observable.
- Fallback: keep `m_tt -> |y_H|` as the strictest first publishable baseline, and frame `pT(H)` as the harder follow-up.

- Risk: the proposal quietly expands again.
- Why acceptable: not acceptable for this run.
- Mitigation: the written baseline forbids adding single-top, jets, or SMEFT until the identifiability test has a clear answer.

12. Follow-up directions only if baseline succeeds

- Add a second `ttbar` family only if it increases captured Higgs variance in a demonstrably orthogonal way.
- Test whether a rank-2 subspace is physically meaningful rather than merely numerically better.
- Only then consider single-top or inclusive jets as stress tests of transferability, not as default ingredients.
- SMEFT belongs strictly after Standard Model identifiability is established; otherwise the program will conflate proton-structure and BSM effects too early.
