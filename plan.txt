1. Core thesis

- Baseline paper: a minimum viable identifiability and closure study for sequential PDF transfer from `ttbar` to Higgs.
- Central question: can one physically meaningful, one-dimensional gluon-PDF subspace, identified from differential `ttbar` data, be transferred to one Higgs observable with predictive power that survives realistic nuisance treatment?
- Strict baseline mapping: `m_tt` (optionally split by `|y_tt|` only if needed for x-localization) -> `|y_H|` in `ggF`.
- Why `|y_H|` and not `pT(H)` in the baseline:
- `|y_H|` is the cleaner first Higgs target for a minimum closure paper because it is directly luminosity-driven and less entangled with radiation-model dependence.
- This makes the baseline test about transferability of proton-structure information, not about whether one can out-model Higgs recoil systematics.
- `pT(H)` remains the first follow-up only after the sequential-fit logic is validated on `|y_H|`.

2. One explicit transferable gluon-PDF subspace

- Commit to one physically meaningful subspace: a single rank-1 gluon-shape mode localized in the overlapping `x-Q^2` region probed by `m_tt` and `ggF` Higgs rapidity.
- Operational definition:
- represent PDF uncertainty with replicas or Hessian responses;
- compute the induced response of the chosen observables;
- extract the leading common mode between `m_tt` and `|y_H|`;
- keep only the mode if it is stable and admits a clear interpretation as a gluon-luminosity deformation, not as a basis artifact.
- Physical interpretation target:
- dominant support in the moderate-`x` gluon region relevant to LHC `gg -> ttbar` and `gg -> H`;
- effective hard scales spanning `Q^2 ~ m_tt^2` on the top side and `Q^2 ~ m_H^2` on the Higgs side;
- transfer means evolution and luminosity projection of the same underlying gluon-shape direction across these scales, not re-fitting unrelated nuisance freedom.
- The paper should show one explicit map or table:
- chosen `m_tt` bins and their dominant `x` support;
- chosen `|y_H|` bins and their dominant `x` support;
- the overlapping `x-Q^2` window that defines the transferable subspace.

3. Minimal scientific claim

- Positive claim: there exists one identifiable gluon-PDF direction that `m_tt` can constrain and that explains a nontrivial fraction of the prior PDF variance of `|y_H|`.
- Negative claim: if this does not hold after nuisance profiling, then low-rank top-to-Higgs transfer is not sequentially valid at baseline and stronger channels or cleaner observables are required.
- Either outcome is publishable because the paper is framed as an identifiability boundary, not a broad analysis platform.

4. Subtasks of the core thesis

- Subtask A: define the overlapping physics support
- Build a compact `x-Q^2` overlap study for the baseline bins.
- Decide whether inclusive `m_tt` is enough or whether one minimal `|y_tt|` split is required to sharpen the overlap.
- Deliverable: a frozen observable definition for the rest of the paper.

- Subtask B: construct the rank-1 transferable mode
- Use theory response matrices for `m_tt` and `|y_H|` under PDF variations.
- Extract one common direction by SVD / generalized eigenmode / canonical-correlation-style compression.
- Reject the mode if it is unstable under mild basis or binning changes.
- Deliverable: one explicit mode with physical interpretation and variance-capture numbers.

- Subtask C: `ttbar` identifiability fit
- Build a `ttbar` likelihood with the rank-1 mode plus a controlled nuisance model.
- Fit pseudo-data and quantify whether the mode is actually learnable from `m_tt`.
- Deliverable: prior-to-posterior contraction for the mode and closure on held-out pseudo-data.

- Subtask D: sequential propagation to Higgs
- Propagate only the posterior of the rank-1 mode into `|y_H|`.
- Quantify how much Higgs PDF variance lies in that direction and how much is reduced after the `ttbar` fit.
- Deliverable: the main sequential-transfer result.

- Subtask E: validity audit
- Stress-test whether the transfer result survives nuisance freedom, alternate theory settings, and null checks.
- Deliverable: an explicit success / failure verdict on sequential-fit validity.

5. Concrete staged execution plan

- Stage 0: freeze the baseline
- Primary top input: absolute and normalized `m_tt`.
- Primary Higgs output: normalized `|y_H|` in `ggF`.
- Optional top refinement only if justified by overlap study: central / forward `|y_tt|` split.
- Not in baseline: `pT(H)`, single-top, inclusive jets, SMEFT.
- Success criterion:
- a single frozen observable chain can be defended from kinematics alone.
- Main risk:
- the overlap in `x` is too broad or too weak for inclusive `m_tt`.
- Fallback:
- add the minimal `|y_tt|` split, but do not introduce a second top process.

- Stage 1: derive the transferable subspace
- Compute observable responses to PDF variations.
- Extract the leading common mode and quantify:
- fraction of `|y_H|` prior PDF variance captured by the mode;
- fraction of `m_tt` prior PDF variance aligned with the same mode.
- Success criterion:
- one stable mode captures a clearly non-negligible fraction of `|y_H|` PDF variance, with a working threshold of `>= 30%` in the chosen fiducial binning.
- Failure criterion:
- no stable rank-1 mode reaches even `~20%` captured Higgs variance across reasonable setup choices.
- Main risk:
- the common structure is diffuse and not compressible.
- Why acceptable:
- this cleanly falsifies the low-rank premise.
- Fallback:
- report a quantitative upper bound on rank-1 transferability; do not silently expand to many modes in the baseline paper.

- Stage 2: closure in `ttbar`
- Generate pseudo-data from held-out PDF members / replicas.
- Fit the rank-1 mode from `m_tt` with nuisance profiling.
- Check estimator bias, posterior calibration, and contraction.
- Success criterion:
- the mode posterior is unbiased in closure and contracts relative to the prior by a practically meaningful amount, e.g. `>= 20%` width reduction for the mode coefficient.
- Failure criterion:
- the mode is too degenerate with scale, shower, detector, or normalization nuisances to be learned.
- Main risk:
- identifiability exists in theory-response space but not in the likelihood.
- Why acceptable:
- this is exactly the distinction the paper should settle.
- Fallback:
- narrow the claim to geometric overlap without inferential identifiability.

- Stage 3: sequential Higgs closure
- Use only the `ttbar` posterior on the rank-1 mode to predict the corresponding posterior for `|y_H|`.
- Compare the predicted Higgs shift / uncertainty reduction to held-out truth in pseudo-data.
- Success criterion:
- posterior predictive intervals for `|y_H|` are calibrated in closure;
- at least `10-20%` reduction of the aligned Higgs PDF variance component is achieved;
- the sign and bin pattern of induced Higgs shifts are correctly predicted for held-out pseudo-data.
- Failure criterion:
- transfer from `ttbar` posterior to Higgs is biased, uncalibrated, or negligible.
- Main risk:
- the mode is learnable in `ttbar` but not relevant enough for Higgs to matter.
- Why acceptable:
- that still sets a direct upper limit on practical transfer.
- Fallback:
- publish the variance-capture ceiling and explain why `|y_H|` is not sufficiently shared with `m_tt`.

- Stage 4: sequential-fit validity audit
- Repeat Stage 3 under expanded nuisance models and modest theory variations.
- Test whether the sequential approach agrees with a joint-truth construction in pseudo-data, within predefined tolerance.
- Success criterion:
- at least half of the nominal Higgs gain from the PDF-only closure survives after nuisance profiling;
- sequential and joint closure results agree within uncertainty for the rank-1 mode coefficient and Higgs posterior in the tested pseudo-data ensemble.
- Failure criterion:
- sequential transfer works only in an idealized PDF-only setting and breaks under realistic nuisance freedom.
- Main risk:
- apparent transfer is an artifact of omitted correlations.
- Why acceptable:
- then the correct scientific conclusion is that a sequential top-control picture is not yet valid at baseline.
- Fallback:
- frame the result as a no-go boundary and specify which missing ingredients would be needed to rescue the idea.

6. Explicit sequential-fit validity criteria

- Validity criterion V1: pre-definition
- the transferable mode is defined before fitting pseudo-data and is not re-optimized on the Higgs target after seeing `ttbar` outcomes.

- Validity criterion V2: sufficiency
- the `ttbar` posterior on the rank-1 mode is sufficient to reproduce the Higgs posterior change seen in a matched closure setup; no hidden extra nuisance tuning is required.

- Validity criterion V3: calibration
- nominal Bayesian / frequentist coverage for the Higgs posterior prediction remains acceptable in pseudo-data closure.

- Validity criterion V4: stability
- the transfer conclusion does not flip under modest perturbative-scale choices, alternate PDF representations, or mild rebinning.

- If any of V1-V4 fails, the paper should explicitly state that the sequential-fit picture is not validated, even if raw correlations look encouraging.

7. Concrete success, failure, and interpretation rules

- Success of the baseline paper means all of the following:
- one stable rank-1 gluon mode is isolated in the overlapping `x-Q^2` region;
- that mode captures a material fraction of prior `|y_H|` PDF variance;
- `m_tt` can constrain that mode in nuisance-aware closure;
- sequential propagation yields calibrated Higgs posterior improvement.

- Partial success means:
- the mode is physically meaningful and relevant for Higgs, but `ttbar` cannot constrain it enough once nuisances are restored.
- Publication angle:
- "common gluon-PDF direction exists, but in-situ transfer is not yet identifiable with baseline top information."

- Informative failure means:
- no stable common mode exists, or sequential closure is invalid even though naive correlations appear strong.
- Publication angle:
- "limits of low-rank top-to-Higgs PDF transfer."

8. Concrete synergy items

- Synergy item 1: theory geometry plus inference
- The work is not just a correlation plot; it connects observable-space geometry to a real likelihood-level identifiability test.

- Synergy item 2: top as control, Higgs as target
- The top channel supplies in-situ information at higher scales; the Higgs channel tests whether that information is truly portable under DGLAP evolution and observable projection.

- Synergy item 3: negative outcomes remain sharp
- The program is scientifically robust because failure yields a real upper bound on what sequential PDF control can achieve in CMS-like analyses.

9. Boundaries and non-goals

- No broad multi-channel platform in the baseline paper.
- No single-top, inclusive jets, or SMEFT in the baseline.
- No automatic escalation to rank-2 unless the professor explicitly asks for a broader second paper.
- No attempt to claim a full Higgs uncertainty reduction program from one study.
- The baseline contribution is narrower and stronger: identify, test, and validate or falsify one transferable gluon-PDF direction.

10. Risk assessment and fallback

- Risk: the chosen `x-Q^2` overlap is physically weak.
- Why acceptable:
- this can be diagnosed early and cheaply.
- Fallback:
- retain `m_tt` but add only the minimal `|y_tt|` split.

- Risk: the extracted mode is numerically unstable.
- Why acceptable:
- then the latent-subspace concept is not mature enough for a baseline paper.
- Fallback:
- publish the instability as evidence against low-rank transfer claims.

- Risk: closure works in PDF-only studies but dies once nuisances are profiled.
- Why acceptable:
- this is the central realism test.
- Fallback:
- make the paper a boundary result on identifiability rather than a positive transfer claim.

- Risk: `|y_H|` is too weakly sensitive for visible practical gain.
- Why acceptable:
- a weak but clean target is still the correct first target for a minimum-validity paper.
- Fallback:
- keep the paper focused on identifiability and reserve `pT(H)` as the next branch if the sequential logic is validated.

11. Follow-up only if the baseline succeeds

- First follow-up: extend the exact same rank-1 mode to `pT(H)` and test whether transfer persists once recoil/radiation sensitivity enters.
- Second follow-up: test whether a second top observable family adds orthogonal information beyond the validated rank-1 mode.
- Third follow-up: only after Standard Model transfer is validated, consider single-top, jets, or SMEFT as structured extensions rather than baseline ingredients.
