1. Core thesis

- Baseline paper: a minimum viable identifiability and closure study for sequential PDF transfer from top-pair production to Higgs production.
- Central question: can one physically meaningful, one-dimensional gluon-PDF latent variable, learned from differential `ttbar` data, be propagated to one Higgs observable with predictive power that survives realistic nuisance treatment?
- Strict baseline mapping:
- top control input: normalized `m_tt` spectrum, with an optional minimal `|y_tt|` split only if the overlap study shows that inclusive `m_tt` is too delocalized in `x`;
- Higgs target output: normalized `|y_H|` in `ggF`.
- Resolution of the baseline-input ambiguity:
- baseline means normalized `m_tt`, not absolute `m_tt`;
- reason: the first paper should test transfer of shape information in a shared gluon-luminosity direction while minimizing contamination from luminosity, acceptance, and overall normalization nuisances;
- absolute `m_tt` is retained only as a robustness cross-check, not as the defining baseline observable.
- Why `|y_H|` and not `pT(H)` in the baseline:
- `|y_H|` is the cleanest first Higgs target because it is driven mainly by parton luminosities and is less entangled with recoil and radiation-model systematics than `pT(H)`;
- this keeps the baseline paper focused on proton-structure transfer rather than on recoil modeling.

2. One explicit transferable gluon-PDF subspace

- Commit to one physically meaningful subspace: a single rank-1 gluon-shape mode localized in the overlapping `x-Q^2` region probed by normalized `m_tt` and normalized `|y_H|`.
- Operational definition:
- represent PDF uncertainty by replicas or Hessian eigenvector responses in the underlying PDF parameter space;
- propagate those variations to the top and Higgs observables to obtain response matrices `R_t` and `R_H`;
- whiten each observable space by its non-PDF covariance so that the extracted mode reflects inferentially useful variation rather than raw bin scales;
- extract the leading shared direction through an SVD / generalized eigenproblem of the whitened cross-response, then map it back to a deformation of the gluon PDF.
- Rank-1 sufficiency test:
- define `z` as the scalar coefficient of the candidate shared mode;
- compare two Higgs posterior constructions in pseudo-data generated from held-out PDF variations:
- sequential rank-1 posterior `p(y_H | p(z | y_t), model)` obtained by fitting `z` only in top data and propagating it forward;
- reference posterior `p(y_H | y_t, model_full)` from the same generative setup but allowing the full retained PDF response space.
- Pre-declared baseline tolerances for calling rank-1 "sufficient":
- posterior mean shift in each Higgs bin within `0.25` of the reference posterior standard deviation;
- posterior variance of each Higgs bin within `10%` relative difference from the reference posterior;
- held-out log predictive density or equivalent proper scoring metric degraded by no more than `10%` relative to the reference posterior.
- These are working tolerances, not laws of nature.
- Why they are defensible at baseline:
- the `0.25 sigma` mean threshold is deliberately tighter than a "visually similar" standard and forces omitted higher-rank PDF structure to remain clearly subdominant to the Higgs posterior width;
- the `10%` variance threshold is a practical ceiling for saying the compressed latent state preserves uncertainty scale rather than merely the central trend;
- the `10%` predictive-score threshold prevents a rank-1 summary from passing while silently mis-shaping the posterior.
- Explicit downgrade rule if these thresholds cannot be defended after the first closure tests:
- keep the continuous diagnostics as the primary result and relabel the verdict from "rank-1 sufficiency established" to "rank-1 approximation quality quantified";
- if the thresholds are too aggressive relative to pseudo-data fluctuations, loosen them once, before main scans, to `0.33 sigma` on means and `15%` on variance and score, and state that the paper is then making a weaker approximate-compression claim rather than a strong sufficiency claim;
- do not re-tune thresholds on the basis of favorable Higgs outcomes.
- Physical interpretation target:
- dominant support in the moderate-`x` gluon region relevant to `gg -> ttbar` and `gg -> H`;
- transfer means evolution and luminosity projection of the same underlying gluon-shape direction across `Q^2 ~ m_tt^2` and `Q^2 ~ m_H^2`, not a refit of unrelated nuisance freedom.
- Required visualization:
- one explicit table or map of `m_tt` bins, their dominant `x` support, `|y_H|` bins, their dominant `x` support, and the overlap window used to define the candidate rank-1 mode.

3. Concrete sequential factorization assumptions

- The sequential strategy must assume a specific factorization, not just "fit top first, then propagate."
- Working statistical factorization:
- let `z` be the scalar shared gluon-PDF mode coefficient;
- let `u_t` be top-specific nuisances and `u_H` Higgs-specific nuisances;
- let `c` denote explicitly propagated common nuisances if any are retained in the baseline construction;
- assume `p(y_t, y_H | z, c, u_t, u_H) = p(y_t | z, c, u_t) p(y_H | z, c, u_H)`;
- assume the prior factorizes as `p(z, c, u_t, u_H) = p(z) p(c) p(u_t) p(u_H)` at baseline;
- assume that all information from top data relevant to Higgs PDF transfer flows through `(z, c)`, meaning `y_H ⟂ y_t | z, c` once the modeled nuisances are conditioned on.
- Baseline nuisance partition must be operational rather than rhetorical:
- top-only nuisance classes:
- `ttbar` acceptance and reconstruction effects specific to the top selection, top-background normalizations, top-specific unfolding / response uncertainties, and top-specific shower / color-reconnection components that do not reappear in the Higgs measurement;
- action: profile them in the top fit, marginalize them out there, and do not propagate them.
- Higgs-only nuisance classes:
- Higgs-category migrations, background-shape uncertainties in the chosen Higgs channel, Higgs-specific efficiency systematics, and Higgs recoil / resolution modeling not shared with the top observable;
- action: include them only in the Higgs likelihood and do not claim any constraint from top data.
- Common nuisance classes to treat explicitly:
- integrated luminosity and overall normalization systematics;
- action: exclude from the baseline transfer claim by using normalized spectra as the primary observables, then verify in the absolute-`m_tt` cross-check that reintroducing normalization mostly affects normalization directions rather than the shared shape mode.
- common PDF-theory ingredients other than `z`, such as residual higher-rank PDF directions or shared `alpha_s` dependence if not frozen;
- action: either include them in the reference model for the sufficiency audit or freeze / bound them and state that the baseline claim is conditional on that restriction.
- common perturbative production uncertainties that can distort both `ttbar` and `ggF`, such as scale-variation-induced shape shifts;
- action: treat them as a common stress-test family in the validity audit, not as part of the propagated baseline latent state; if they materially correlate the channels after conditioning on `z`, the baseline sequential claim fails.
- common detector-calibration classes that could survive normalization, especially jet energy scale / resolution, `b`-tagging, and lepton efficiencies if the eventual concrete measurements share them;
- action: test each class with nuisance-on / nuisance-off response studies; if a class induces aligned top and Higgs distortions comparable to the PDF mode, either promote it into `c` and propagate it jointly or remove it from the baseline claim by choosing measurement definitions where it is negligible.
- Sequential validity therefore means two things at once:
- modeling validity: the factorization above is a good approximation for the chosen baseline observables;
- inferential sufficiency: the scalar `z`, plus any explicitly retained `c`, retains essentially all top-derived information that matters for the Higgs posterior.

4. Minimal scientific claim

- Positive claim: there exists one identifiable gluon-PDF direction that normalized `m_tt` can constrain and that explains a material fraction of the prior PDF variance of normalized `|y_H|`.
- Stronger positive claim, only if supported by the sufficiency test: the posterior over that single direction is approximately sufficient for the Higgs posterior change induced by top data.
- Negative claim: if this does not hold after nuisance profiling or if the reference full-space posterior contains important additional structure, then low-rank sequential transfer is not valid at baseline.
- Either outcome is publishable because the paper is framed as an identifiability boundary, not as a guaranteed Higgs-uncertainty reduction story.

5. Subtasks of the core thesis

- Subtask A: define the overlapping physics support
- build a compact `x-Q^2` overlap study for normalized `m_tt` and normalized `|y_H|`;
- decide whether inclusive `m_tt` is enough or whether one minimal `|y_tt|` split is needed to sharpen the overlap;
- deliverable: a frozen observable definition for the rest of the paper.

- Subtask B: early normalized-vs-absolute `m_tt` diagnostic
- build the leading shared-mode extraction twice, once with normalized `m_tt` and once with absolute `m_tt`, using the same Higgs target and the same PDF response basis;
- compare the overlap map, leading singular value, Higgs-variance capture, and bin-wise Higgs response pattern between the two top inputs;
- define "shared mode survives normalization" as: the normalized-spectrum mode captures at least `80%` of the Higgs-variance fraction captured by the absolute-spectrum mode, and the induced Higgs-bin response has cosine similarity above `0.9` after whitening;
- deliverable: one figure and one table establishing that normalization removes mostly normalization-like information rather than the shared shape mode.

- Subtask C: construct the candidate rank-1 mode
- compute theory response matrices for the chosen observables under PDF variations;
- whiten by non-PDF covariance and extract the leading shared mode;
- test basis stability under Hessian vs replica representations and under mild rebinning;
- deliverable: one explicit mode with physical interpretation and variance-capture numbers.

- Subtask D: run the rank-1 sufficiency audit
- define the reference retained PDF space, for example the top few singular directions needed to saturate nearly all shared Higgs-relevant variance;
- compare rank-1 sequential propagation against that reference in held-out pseudo-data;
- deliverable: a verdict with two layers:
- primary: whether the pre-declared sufficiency thresholds are passed;
- fallback: if not, a continuous approximation-quality statement with no inflated sufficiency claim.

- Subtask E: `ttbar` identifiability fit
- build a top likelihood with `z` plus a controlled nuisance model;
- fit pseudo-data and quantify whether `z` is learnable from normalized `m_tt`;
- deliverable: prior-to-posterior contraction, bias tests, and calibration for `z`.

- Subtask F: sequential propagation to Higgs
- propagate only the posterior of `z` into normalized `|y_H|`;
- quantify how much Higgs PDF variance lies in that direction and how much is reduced after the top fit;
- deliverable: the main top-to-Higgs transfer result.

- Subtask G: validity audit under realism
- stress-test whether the transfer result survives nuisance freedom, alternate theory settings, and null checks;
- deliverable: an explicit success or failure verdict on sequential-fit validity.

6. Concrete staged execution plan

- Stage 0: freeze the baseline
- primary top input: normalized `m_tt`;
- primary Higgs output: normalized `|y_H|` in `ggF`;
- optional top refinement only if justified by overlap study: central / forward `|y_tt|` split;
- robustness cross-check, not baseline: absolute `m_tt`;
- not in baseline: `pT(H)`, single-top, inclusive jets, SMEFT.
- Success criterion:
- a single observable chain can be defended from kinematics and nuisance structure.
- Main risk:
- normalized `m_tt` may discard too much useful information.
- Why acceptable:
- the first paper is about clean transfer logic, not maximum sensitivity.
- Fallback:
- include absolute `m_tt` only as a comparison study, while keeping normalized `m_tt` as the baseline definition.

- Stage 1: prove the shared mode survives normalization
- run the normalized-vs-absolute `m_tt` diagnostic before any main closure campaign;
- inspect whether normalization preserves the leading shared Higgs-relevant direction and merely removes normalization-dominated components.
- Success criterion:
- normalized `m_tt` passes the survival criterion against absolute `m_tt`, or fails only in overall impact size while preserving the same Higgs-response pattern.
- Failure criterion:
- normalization qualitatively rotates away the leading shared Higgs-relevant direction.
- Main risk:
- the normalized observable becomes too orthogonal to the useful PDF mode.
- Why acceptable:
- this is an early kill test that prevents an over-built proposal on the wrong baseline.
- Fallback:
- explicitly downgrade the baseline claim to "shape-only transfer is too weak," and keep absolute `m_tt` as a diagnostic side study rather than silently switching baselines.

- Stage 2: derive the candidate shared mode
- compute observable responses to PDF variations and non-PDF covariance;
- extract the leading shared mode and quantify:
- fraction of prior Higgs PDF variance captured by rank-1;
- fraction of prior top PDF variance aligned with the same mode;
- stability under PDF basis choice and mild rebinning.
- Reporting rule for numerical thresholds:
- do not present `30%` as a hard physical threshold;
- relabel it as a working materiality benchmark: rank-1 is "material" if it captures roughly one-third or more of the prior Higgs PDF variance in the chosen baseline observable, because below that level the ceiling on practical Higgs impact is likely too small for a standalone baseline paper;
- also report the continuous number, not just pass/fail.
- Failure rule:
- if the captured Higgs variance is consistently small, for example well below the materiality benchmark across reasonable choices, the baseline rank-1 program is too weak.
- Main risk:
- the common structure is diffuse and not compressible.
- Fallback:
- publish a quantitative upper bound on rank-1 transferability rather than expanding the scope opportunistically.

- Stage 3: rank-1 sufficiency test
- define a richer reference PDF-response space that is still low-dimensional and numerically stable;
- generate held-out pseudo-data from that reference space;
- compare sequential rank-1 Higgs posteriors to reference Higgs posteriors using the pre-declared sufficiency tolerances.
- Success criterion:
- rank-1 reproduces the reference Higgs posterior to within the declared working tolerances across the pseudo-data ensemble.
- Failure criterion:
- higher-rank PDF structure changes Higgs means, variances, or predictive scores beyond tolerance.
- Main risk:
- the leading mode is real but not sufficient.
- Why acceptable:
- this is exactly the boundary between "nice correlation picture" and "valid sequential latent variable."
- Fallback:
- relabel the program from "rank-1 sufficient transfer" to "leading-mode diagnostic transfer," or broaden to rank-2 only in a clearly separate follow-up paper.

- Stage 4: closure in `ttbar`
- generate pseudo-data from held-out PDF members or replicas;
- fit `z` from normalized `m_tt` with nuisance profiling;
- check estimator bias, posterior calibration, and contraction.
- Reporting rule for contraction:
- do not present `20%` prior-width reduction as a theorem;
- relabel it as a practical learnability benchmark, meaning the posterior tightening is large enough to matter downstream;
- always quote the actual contraction factor with uncertainty.
- Failure criterion:
- `z` is too degenerate with scale, shower, detector, or normalization nuisances to be learned with useful precision.
- Main risk:
- identifiability exists in response geometry but not in the likelihood.
- Fallback:
- narrow the claim to overlap geometry plus a no-go inference result.

- Stage 5: sequential Higgs closure
- use only the top posterior on `z` to predict the corresponding posterior for normalized `|y_H|`;
- compare the predicted Higgs shift and uncertainty reduction to held-out truth in pseudo-data.
- Reporting rule for impact thresholds:
- the earlier `10-20%` Higgs-variance reduction should be relabeled as an aspirational impact range, not a requirement for scientific validity;
- the real requirement is calibrated posterior prediction plus non-negligible transferred information;
- if the gain is only a few percent but robust and well understood, that can still support a boundary paper, though not a strong phenomenology claim.
- Success criterion:
- posterior predictive intervals for `|y_H|` are calibrated in closure;
- the sign and bin pattern of induced Higgs shifts are correctly predicted;
- the transferred information is clearly above numerical noise and consistent with the Stage 2 variance-capture ceiling.
- Main risk:
- the mode is learnable in top data but too weakly relevant for Higgs to matter.
- Fallback:
- publish the practical ceiling on top-to-Higgs transfer for `|y_H|`.

- Stage 6: sequential-fit validity audit
- repeat Stage 5 under expanded nuisance models and modest theory variations;
- compare sequential closure with a matched joint construction in pseudo-data.
- Required common-nuisance actions in this stage:
- luminosity / normalization family: confirm normalized-baseline results are unchanged when these nuisances are switched off or floated only in the absolute cross-check;
- common perturbative-theory family: add as correlated stress-test nuisances and check whether residual top-Higgs dependence remains after conditioning on `z`;
- common detector-calibration family: test each candidate class one at a time, then in combination, and promote only genuinely non-negligible survivors into a propagated common latent block `c`.
- Success criterion:
- the qualitative conclusion about transfer survives nuisance enrichment and modest theory changes;
- sequential and joint constructions agree for the Higgs posterior within the same working tolerance scale used in the sufficiency test.
- Failure criterion:
- apparent transfer is an artifact of omitted correlations or over-aggressive factorization.
- Main risk:
- sequential transfer works only in an idealized PDF-only limit.
- Fallback:
- frame the result as a no-go boundary and specify which missing ingredients would have to be promoted into the propagated latent state.

7. Explicit sequential-fit validity criteria

- V1: pre-definition
- the shared mode, the survival diagnostic, and all sufficiency tolerances are fixed before fitting pseudo-data and are not tuned on Higgs outcomes.

- V2: factorization validity
- the chosen nuisance partition and likelihood factorization are documented and survive direct stress tests; any common nuisance not propagated must be shown to be negligible for the baseline claim.

- V3: survival under normalization
- the leading shared Higgs-relevant mode extracted from normalized `m_tt` is demonstrably aligned with the one seen in absolute `m_tt`, within the pre-declared survival metric.

- V4: rank-1 sufficiency
- the scalar latent variable `z`, with any explicitly retained `c`, reproduces the reference Higgs posterior to within the declared working tolerances in held-out pseudo-data.

- V5: calibration
- the sequential Higgs posterior has acceptable coverage or posterior calibration in closure.

- V6: stability
- the transfer conclusion does not flip under mild rebinning, alternate PDF parameterizations, or modest perturbative variations.

- If any of V1-V6 fails, the paper should state clearly that the baseline sequential rank-1 picture is not validated.

8. Concrete success, failure, and interpretation rules

- Full success means all of the following:
- one stable rank-1 gluon mode is isolated in the overlapping `x-Q^2` region;
- that mode survives the normalized-vs-absolute `m_tt` diagnostic;
- the mode captures a material fraction of prior Higgs PDF variance;
- rank-1 passes the explicit sufficiency test against the richer reference space;
- normalized `m_tt` can constrain that mode in nuisance-aware closure;
- sequential propagation yields calibrated Higgs posterior improvement.

- Partial success means:
- a physically meaningful shared mode exists, but either rank-1 sufficiency fails or top data cannot constrain the mode enough once nuisances are restored.
- Publication angle:
- "shared gluon structure exists, but a one-parameter sequential transfer is not yet validated."

- Informative failure means:
- no stable common mode exists, normalization destroys the useful mode, or the factorization / sufficiency assumptions fail even though naive correlations look strong.
- Publication angle:
- "limits of low-rank top-to-Higgs PDF transfer."

9. Concrete synergy items

- Synergy item 1: theory geometry plus inference
- the program connects observable-space response geometry to a likelihood-level sufficiency test rather than stopping at correlation plots.

- Synergy item 2: top as control, Higgs as target
- top data probe the shared gluon direction at higher scales; Higgs data test whether that same direction remains predictive after evolution and nuisance-aware projection.

- Synergy item 3: a sharp negative result is still valuable
- failure of rank-1 sufficiency, failure of normalization survival, or failure of sequential factorization is scientifically informative because it sets a concrete limit on what "top controls Higgs PDF uncertainty" can mean in practice.

10. Boundaries and non-goals

- No broad multi-channel platform in the baseline paper.
- No single-top, inclusive jets, or SMEFT in the baseline.
- No automatic escalation to rank-2 if rank-1 fails; that becomes a separate, explicitly broadened follow-up.
- No claim that one study solves Higgs PDF uncertainties in general.
- The baseline contribution is narrower and stronger: identify, test, and validate or falsify one sequentially propagated gluon-PDF latent variable.

11. Risk assessment and fallback

- Risk: normalized `m_tt` is too shape-insensitive or too delocalized in `x`.
- Why acceptable:
- this can be diagnosed early in the overlap and normalization-survival study.
- Fallback:
- add the minimal `|y_tt|` split, while keeping the top observable family fixed.

- Risk: the extracted shared mode is numerically unstable.
- Why acceptable:
- instability itself argues against a robust latent-variable claim.
- Fallback:
- publish the instability as evidence that low-rank transfer is not yet well defined.

- Risk: rank-1 is real but not sufficient.
- Why acceptable:
- that still distinguishes "leading direction" from "adequate sequential summary," which is a scientifically useful result.
- Fallback:
- relabel the outcome as a diagnostic shared mode and reserve multi-mode transfer for follow-up work.

- Risk: closure works in PDF-only studies but fails once nuisances are profiled.
- Why acceptable:
- this is the key realism test the proposal should confront directly.
- Fallback:
- convert the paper into a boundary result on identifiability under realistic nuisance structure.

- Risk: `|y_H|` is too weakly sensitive for visible phenomenological gain.
- Why acceptable:
- a weak but clean target is still the right first target for validating the sequential logic.
- Fallback:
- keep the paper focused on validity and practical ceilings, then move to `pT(H)` only after the logic is validated.

- Risk: the chosen sufficiency thresholds are too strict or too loose to be persuasive.
- Why acceptable:
- the plan now treats them as pre-declared working tolerances with an explicit downgrade path, not as hidden pass/fail magic numbers.
- Fallback:
- report continuous approximation-quality metrics as the main result and avoid an over-claimed sufficiency verdict.

12. Follow-up only if the baseline succeeds

- First follow-up: extend the same validated latent mode to `pT(H)` and test whether transfer survives once recoil and radiation sensitivity enter.
- Second follow-up: test whether adding a second top observable family provides genuinely orthogonal information beyond the validated rank-1 mode.
- Third follow-up: only after Standard Model transfer is validated, consider single-top, inclusive jets, or SMEFT as structured extensions rather than baseline ingredients.
