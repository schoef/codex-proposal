- Flagship question: Can CMS extract a competitive internal constraint on the medium-to-large-`x` gluon PDF in the kinematic region probed by high-mass and boosted `t tbar` production, using detector-level unbinned inference in dileptonic top events with realistic nuisance profiling?

- Program spine: The proposal is centered on one CMS measurement program, not on a broad "AI for proton structure" agenda. The core deliverable is a publication-quality `t tbar -> dilepton` SBI-PDF analysis, benchmarked against strong binned baselines and followed by one concrete downstream propagation study.

- Central claim: The novelty is the combination of detector-level PDF inference, explicit nuisance profiling, and no forced compression to a small histogram basis. The proposal should claim primarily a more robust treatment of information and systematics in a realistic CMS measurement, while leaving open whether the gain appears as better compression, stronger profiling, or a qualitatively new constraint in selected kinematic regions.

- Flagship measurement: Use CMS Run II dileptonic `t tbar` events as the first full data application, with limited Run III integration only after the flagship channel is validated. This channel is experimentally mature, gluon dominated, and already supported by established CMS object definitions, triggers, and differential analyses.

- Physics sensitivity: Focus the gluon argument on a limited set of reconstructed response directions that should remain informative after profiling, such as the `t tbar` invariant mass, the top-system transverse momentum or recoil, rapidity-sensitive observables linked to partonic `x`, and correlated lepton-plus-`b`-jet kinematics that retain acceptance information. The proposal should explain why these directions are not fully saturated by existing few-bin differential measurements once detector effects and nuisance correlations are treated explicitly.

- Kinematic positioning: State the physics target modestly and precisely. The aim is not to make dileptonic tops the general "primary calibration sample" for the gluon PDF, but to provide a CMS-internal, process-local constraint complementary to global fits in the medium-to-large-`x` region relevant for high-mass and boosted top production.

- Statistical framework: Infer a restricted gluon-PDF parameter basis together with the dominant experimental and theory nuisance parameters inside one detector-level surrogate likelihood. Define success operationally: closure on simulation and Asimov tests, stable nuisance pulls, and measurable improvement over a conventional binned baseline built from the strongest existing CMS-style differential observables.

- Experimental realism: Include one explicitly CMS-facing paragraph on the analysis burden: dilepton triggers, lepton and jet reconstruction, missing transverse momentum, `b` tagging, background composition, era handling, top reconstruction choices, and the dominant nuisance families including JES/JER, `b` tagging, recoil, luminosity, backgrounds, and theory-scale variations. The proposal becomes stronger when it shows that the measurement is reviewable inside collaboration practice, not only statistically elegant.

- Validation logic: The measurement should be anchored to continuity with existing CMS top differential analyses rather than presented as a replacement for them. Benchmark the unbinned likelihood against strong histogram-based baselines, show where profiling changes the result, and define the intended public artifact early, for example a compact likelihood interface or response basis that others in CMS can validate and reuse.

- Work-package structure: Stage the project brutally realistically. First deliver Run II simulation closure and Asimov studies. Then perform the Run II data measurement in the flagship dilepton channel. Then add a limited Run III extension if the analysis machinery is stable. Only after that should the program spend effort on portability to another process.

- Downstream propagation study: Keep one concrete Higgs deliverable, framed narrowly as a propagation test to an existing CMS differential gluon-fusion measurement with a restricted observable set. The point is to demonstrate portability of the top-constrained posterior to a real CMS analysis object, not to launch a second flagship measurement program.

- Deliverable hierarchy: Core deliverables are: one detector-level `t tbar` dilepton gluon constraint in CMS, a publication-quality comparison to a strong binned baseline, and one downstream propagation study. Optional extensions should be clearly separated so the proposal remains fundable even if later layers move more slowly.

- Optional extension: A restricted single-top study can remain only as a contingent extension, tied to one exact topology and one exact PDF question. It should not sit in the core narrative unless the proposal can show that it adds a clearly distinct gluon or heavy-flavor lever arm beyond further `t tbar` phase-space coverage.

- Optional extension: A PDF-plus-SMEFT study should be demoted to a later interpretation layer. Its justification is only credible if framed as a degeneracy stress test on the same `t tbar` observables used in the flagship measurement, not as a separate simultaneous top-PDF-SMEFT program.

- Risk management: If the gain over strong binned baselines becomes modest once full CMS nuisances are included, the fallback result is still a valuable detector-level likelihood framework with validated nuisance treatment, a precise statement of where unbinned inference helps or does not help, and a reusable CMS analysis object for downstream interpretations.

- Expected impact: The strongest impact claim is that CMS can produce a scientifically differentiated, experimentally credible, detector-level gluon-sensitive top measurement that is complementary to global PDF fitting and directly useful for one downstream precision analysis. That is a sharper and more defensible outcome than claiming a broad top-centered program across proton structure, Higgs, and SMEFT all at once.
